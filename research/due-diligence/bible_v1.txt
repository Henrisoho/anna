How to use this book
This is a build-to-institutional-depth playbook set. Each chapter gives a data room index, a diligence request
list by workstream, metrics with cited acceptable ranges, real failure cases with the specific check that
catches each, red flags, and hard questions. The bar is a lender or sponsor data room, not a blog checklist.

     THE SPINE

     Verify the sponsor, verify the cash, verify the collateral or the earnings, verify the legal title, and
     separate what you confirmed from what you could not.

The single most repeated failure mode across real estate, funds, and credit is one person controlling money
movement with no independent administrator, custodian, or proof of cash. That one structural check would
have caught Madoff, Drive Planning, and most of the 2024 real estate Ponzi cases.
THREE USES
• The standard. Every posted deal is built and checked against the relevant chapter.
• The first pass. Chapter 0 plus the deal-type chapter is the brief for an AI first-pass review.
• The red team. Appendix A holds the prompt for attacking a draft deck before members see it.

READING THE MARKS
•   WEAK marks a figure that rests on vendor, aggregator, or practitioner-only sourcing. Confirm it before it
    anchors a posted deal.
• CHECK: marks the specific test that catches a failure mode.
• Sources appear in parentheses beside each claim and in full at the back. Practitioner and institutional
    sources outrank marketing content.

0.1 How top firms run a diligence process
The sequence is the same at every institutional shop. What changes by deal type is the depth of each
workstream.



       1                     2             3                  4                  5                6                 7
     Screen            LOI or term     Confirmatory       Third-party       IC memo and         Docs and         Monitoring
                         sheet           diligence          reports             vote             funding




FIGURE 1 The institutional diligence sequence. Steps 3 and 4 are where deals are won or lost.

1     Screen. One-page thesis, kill tests, mandate fit. Reject fast.
2     Indication of interest, LOI, or term sheet with exclusivity.
3     Confirmatory diligence in parallel workstreams: financial, commercial, legal, tax, operational,
      technical, management and background, regulatory, insurance, environmental, ESG, IT and cyber.
4     Third-party reports commissioned: QoE, appraisal, environmental, independent engineer,
      background checks.
5     Investment committee memo and vote.
6     Final documentation, conditions precedent, funding.
7     Post-close monitoring and covenant tracking.
Buyout confirmatory diligence typically runs weeks 9 to 16 of a process and covers Quality of Earnings,
legal, tax, IT, commercial, environmental, and insurance (ctacquisitions.com). Leveraged transactions often
run 60 to 90 days between signing and closing to accommodate lender underwriting (windsordrake.com).


0.2 Sponsor and management background checks
• Identity verification: government ID, entity formation documents.
• Full background check: criminal, civil litigation, bankruptcy, regulatory (SEC, FINRA BrokerCheck,
    state securities), judgments and liens, adverse news.
• Prior track record verified independently, not from the sponsor's own deck. Confirm claimed exits,
    AUM, and prior deal outcomes.
• Resume verification. In the Drive Planning and Toltec Capital SEC cases, the principals inflated their
    resumes to lure investors (therealdeal.com; SEC). Resume inflation is a documented fraud precursor.

0.3 KYC, AML, and sanctions
Applies to every investor and every counterparty.
• Customer identification (CIP): government ID or entity formation documents, proof of address.
• Beneficial ownership: identify and verify every natural person owning or controlling 25% or more of
    an entity investor, plus any control person (FinCEN CDD Rule, 31 CFR 1010.230).
• Sanctions screening: every investor and beneficial owner against OFAC SDN and sectoral lists, plus
    UN, EU, and UK lists for international parties, at onboarding and continuously (getcaruso.com;
    carta.com).
• PEP and adverse-media screening.
• Source of funds AND source of wealth. These are different. A single bank statement is not a
    source-of-wealth narrative.
• Accredited investor or qualified purchaser verification. Under Rule 506(c), actual documentary
    evidence is required (tax returns, W-2s, bank statements, or a licensed professional's letter), not
    self-certification. Third-party verification letters are generally current for only 90 days
    (augment.market; checkfile.ai).


0.4 Fraud detection: the universal red flags

   RED FLAG: ONE PERSON                                              CLEAN: SEPARATED                  + independent auditor



                                GP                                       Manager        Bank / custodian     Administrator




        Decides              Moves cash      Reports                     Decides          Moves cash           Reports



FIGURE 2 Segregation of duties. When one person decides, moves cash, and reports, nothing else in the file can be trusted.

• One person controls investment decisions, cash movement, and reporting. No segregation of duties is
    the single most consistent precursor to operational failure and fraud (thomasmurray.com).
• No independent administrator, custodian, or auditor, or an auditor too small for the mandate. Madoff's
    auditor, Friehling and Horowitz, was a two-partner firm with no peer review (academia.edu;
    thehedgefundjournal.com).
• Guaranteed or fixed above-market returns. Drive Planning promised 10% every six months on a "bridge
    loan." The Adam brothers promised up to 13.5% monthly (SEC 2024-97, 2024-107).
• Refusal to allow on-site diligence or direct contact with the administrator, auditor, or prime broker.
• Statements produced by the manager rather than an independent third party.

• New-investor money paying old-investor returns. This is the definition of a Ponzi. Verify with proof of

0.5 Verification of funds and assets
• Proof of cash. Reconcile reported revenue and earnings back to bank statements. Standard in QoE. It
      catches what the P&L hides, which is why the data request asks for 36 months of statements, not a trial
      balance (notveryprivateequity.com; duedilio.com).
• Independent confirmation of bank balances, custody statements, and asset existence directly from the
      third party, unsolicited.
• Title and lien search for real assets. UCC search for personal property.
• For funds: call the administrator, auditor, and prime broker directly (dealroom.net).

0.8 The universal deck order
What investors read, in the order they read it.
1     One line: what it is and the ask.
2     Problem or opportunity.
3     The asset or business.
4     Sponsor and track record.
5     Market.
6     The numbers. Historical first, then projection.
7     Structure, use of proceeds, security.
8     Returns with sensitivities.
9     Risks and mitigants.
10    Terms.

8.2 The 280E anchor
This is the central diligence issue.

                     NORMAL BUSINESS                                          UNDER 280E




                             COGS                                                 COGS




                     Operating expenses                                     Operating expenses   Taxed federally: opex is
                                                                                                 not deductible
                                           Taxed federally
                             Profit                                               Profit


FIGURE 14 How 280E changes the federal tax base. Schematic, not to scale.

• IRC 280E denies all ordinary business deductions and credits to a business "trafficking" in a Schedule I
    or II controlled substance. Only COGS under IRC 471 is deductible (cannabisindustrylawyer.com;
    greengrowthcpas.com). Effective federal rates can exceed 70% (peasebell.com).
• Harborside (Tax Court 2018, 9th Cir. 2021): 280E applies to state-legal cannabis. COGS is limited to
    471(a) direct costs. 263A is not available (greengrowthcpas.com; cannabizguide.com).
• April 2026: FDA-approved marijuana products and marijuana under a state medical license moved to
    Schedule III, which can lift 280E for medical-only operators. Adult-use remains Schedule I and under
    280E (getmeadow.com; greengrowthcpas.com). Verify current federal status and IRS guidance per deal.
• State conformity varies. Some states (for example California) conform to 280E. Others (Colorado,
    Oregon, New York, New Jersey, Minnesota) decouple and allow state deductions (bluecloudcpa.com;
    greengrowthcpas.com).


8.3 Diligence checklist
• License. Verify it is real, current, in good standing, and transferable. Confirm with the state regulator.
    Check ownership-change approval requirements.
• 280E tax exposure. Model the real effective tax. Verify COGS methodology under 471. Check dual
    medical and adult-use apportionment.
• State compliance. Seed-to-sale tracking, testing, packaging and labeling, security.
• Banking. How cash is handled. Banking relationship or cash-management risk.
• Real estate. Local approval, zoning, landlord consent.

10.1 How it works

• Appendix A lists 89 requested documents (mattercite.com).
• LPs spend the most time on Track Record, Fund Terms, and Governance / Risk / Compliance, where a

10.4 Diligence checklist
• Track record: gross-to-net, PME, loss ratio, DPI and TVPI, attribution to the current team, realized
    versus unrealized marks.
• Fund terms versus ILPA Principles: fees, carry, waterfall (deal-by-deal versus whole-fund), clawback,
    GP commit, key-person and no-fault-divorce provisions.
• Alignment: GP commitment size, team compensation and retention.
• Governance: valuation policy, conflicts, LPAC, subscription-line usage and its effect on reported IRR.
• Operations: administrator, auditor, custody, cyber, business continuity.


10.5 The numbers
• Scrutinize IRR with and without the subscription credit facility. A facility can inflate reported IRR
    materially.
• Benchmark the track record against vintage-year peers and a PME.
• Do not accept gross IRR alone.


10.6 Failure modes
• Self-administration and no independent NAV (Madoff). CHECK: independent administrator, and call
    them.
• Subscription-line-inflated IRR. CHECK: recompute without the facility.
• Style drift: strategy not matching 13F filings or the stated mandate. CHECK: reconcile holdings to strategy.


10.7 Red flags
• No independent administrator or custodian.
• Auditor too small or recently changed.
• Refusal of direct service-provider contact or an on-site visit.
• Guaranteed or suspiciously smooth returns.
