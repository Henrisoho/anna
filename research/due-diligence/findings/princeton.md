## 6 Energy and Infrastructure

### 6.4 Diligence checklist (additions)

- PRE-FID CAPITAL IS 100% AT RISK. Pre-FID development spend (interconnection studies, permitting, site control, deposits) is typically incurred 2 to 10 years before COD and is fully lost if the project does not reach FID. Net-Zero America sizes it at about $185B to $190B against about $2.6T of supply-side capital committed in the 2020s (roughly 7%). CHECK: for any development-stage deal, split the capital stack into pre-FID, construction (pre-COD) and operating dollars and price each separately; never underwrite pre-FID dollars at a construction-loan or operating-asset return. [NEW] (Net-Zero America Final Report Summary, Oct 2021, "Capital mobilization" section, https://netzeroamerica.princeton.edu/img/Princeton%20NZA%20FINAL%20REPORT%20SUMMARY%20(29Oct2021).pdf)
- "Risk capital" runs to COD, not to financial close. Princeton defines risk capital as pre-FID capital plus everything committed before the Commercial Operation Date, exposed to development, market, construction and technology-performance risk. CHECK: the model shows a cash-at-risk curve by month through COD, and the IC memo states the maximum cumulative exposure before first revenue. [STRENGTHENS 6.1] (same source)
- Build-rate realism test. Net-Zero America's constrained case capped US wind plus solar additions at 35 GW per year, described as about 30% above the single-year historical record; the historical solar-plus-wind peak used was 21 GW per year (2015). REPEAT later modeled solar needing 44 to 51 GW per year (2023 to 2030) against 19 GW installed in 2021. CHECK: when a sponsor's market thesis assumes national build rates far above any historical year, treat the demand side (EPC crews, transformers, modules, interconnection approvals) as the binding constraint and haircut the schedule. [NEW] (NZA Final Report Summary; Utility Dive on REPEAT transmission report, https://www.utilitydive.com/news/repeat-project-carbon-emissions-transmission-report/687446/)
- Transmission is the system bottleneck. REPEAT (Princeton ZERO Lab) finds US transmission has expanded about 1% per year; it must rise about 50% faster just to carry current-policy wind and solar growth, and about double for a 2035 net-zero target. If transmission stays at ~1% per year, low-carbon share falls from about 75% to about 61% of 2030 generation and roughly half of the expected emissions reduction is forfeited. CHECK: any project whose economics need a new or upgraded line (network upgrades in the LGIA, a merchant HVDC line, a REZ) gets a "no new transmission" downside case with curtailment and basis-risk applied. [STRENGTHENS 6.4 POWER AND GRID] (Utility Dive, REPEAT report; REPEAT Project, https://repeatproject.org)
- Net-Zero America scenario sizing: transmission capacity about doubles by 2050 in the least-cost case, rises to about 5x in the 100% renewables case, and grows 60% by 2030 in the high-electrification case (interim). Use as a macro frame only; do not cite as project-level demand. [NEW, context] (NZA Final Report Summary; NZA Annex F HV Transmission, https://netzeroamerica.princeton.edu/img/NZA%20Annex%20F%20-%20HV%20Transmission.pdf)
- Models assume perfect foresight; your deal does not. NZA explicitly warns its least-cost pathways assume "rational and efficient markets" with investors responding instantly, while real capital mobilization needs long lead times under deep uncertainty (technology cost, future policy, public acceptance). CHECK: when a sponsor cites a national decarbonization or load-growth model as its demand case, ask which assumptions (perfect foresight, unconstrained siting, unlimited transmission) the project actually depends on. [NEW] (NZA Final Report Summary)

### 6.4 Data-center power: flexible interconnection (new subsection)

- FLEXIBLE INTERCONNECTION PLUS BRING-YOUR-OWN-CAPACITY (BYOC). Primary report: Brancucci, Cutler and Jenkins, "Flexible Data Centers: A Faster, More Affordable Path to Power" (Camus, encoord and Princeton ZERO Lab, Dec 2025; funded by Google, which reviewed it before publication). Baseline: data centers face 3 to 7 years to connect against an 18 to 24 month build. Modeling six real 500 MW candidate sites in one PJM utility across all 8,760 hours: four were transmission-constrained; with flexible connection plus BYOC a 500 MW campus reaches full operation in about 2 years, 3 to 5 years faster. Grid power was available more than 99% of hours; on-site resources (batteries, generators, compute flexibility) dispatched 40 to 70 hours a year; transmission curtailment ran 7 to 35 hours a year in events of 4 to 16 hours, with peak curtailment up to 281 MW at the worst site; generation shortfalls added about 32 hours a year, concentrated in extreme weather. Economics per GW of new data-center load: firm-only service adds about $764M of annual system supply cost (2.17 GW of nameplate additions); 20% conditional-firm service avoids 273 MW of new build ($78M); BYOC internalizes $326M of capacity cost; energy payments cover $329M; net, the data center covers about $733M per GW. Most tariffs today offer only firm or non-firm service, often no non-firm option at all, so the structure frequently does not yet exist. [NEW] (Camus/encoord/Princeton ZERO Lab whitepaper, https://www.rtoinsider.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Whitepaper-dec-2025.pdf; Canary Media summary, https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/data-centers/pjm-needs-flexible-data-centers)
- CHECK for a data-center power deal claiming a "fast-track" grid connection: obtain the utility's written flexible / non-firm interconnection terms, the hourly curtailment study (hours per year, MW curtailed, which hours), and evidence that the on-site generation and storage are sized to cover the curtailment window. A non-firm connection is a different collateral from firm service: the hyperscaler lease must tolerate curtailment, or the BYOC assets must be in the security package. [NEW]
- RED FLAG: a sponsor citing the Princeton/Camus result as proof its site will connect in two years. The study covered six PJM sites in one utility territory, was funded by Google, and critics dispute that AI loads will actually curtail. Ask for the site-specific study. [NEW]
- HARD QUESTION: Is your interconnection firm or flexible? How many hours per year can the utility curtail you, and what carries the load during those hours? [NEW]

## 10 Funds and Co-Investments

### 10.5 The numbers (additions)

- DATABASE BIAS HAIRCUT. Using TASS data 1994 to 2003, Malkiel and Saha (Princeton CEPS) found backfilled hedge-fund returns averaged over 500 bp per year above contemporaneously reported returns; live funds out-earned dead funds by almost 750 bp per year (1996 to 2003); and surviving funds averaged 13.50% versus 9.71% for all funds, a 379 bp survivorship gap. Of 604 funds reporting in 1996, fewer than 25% (124) still existed in 2004. A separate estimate (Posthuma and van der Sluis) puts end-of-life non-reporting bias at over 600 bp per year. LTCM lost 92% of its capital and reported none of it to the databases. CHECK: any peer benchmark or "industry average" in a manager deck must state whether it excludes backfill and includes dead funds; otherwise haircut the benchmark by several hundred bp before comparing. [NEW] (Malkiel and Saha, "Hedge Funds: Risk and Return," Princeton CEPS Working Paper 104, https://gceps.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/104malkiel.pdf; published FAJ 2005, https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v61y2005i6p80-88.html)
- PERSISTENCE IS CLOSE TO A COIN FLIP FOR HEDGE FUNDS. In the same study, the share of one year's above-average funds that repeated the next year was about 50% (about 55% if funds that stopped reporting are excluded rather than counted as losers); a top-quartile fund had about a 50% chance of being above average the next year, and about one third stayed top quartile. Fund-of-funds returned less than the hedge-fund universe after the second fee layer. CHECK: do not accept "top-quartile" status as evidence of skill for hedge or liquid-alternatives managers; require attribution and a factor or put-writing benchmark (below). [NEW] (same source)
- THE PUT-WRITING BENCHMARK. Jurek (Princeton) and Stafford show the aggregate hedge-fund index's risk (beta, drawdowns, volatility) is matched by a mechanical S&P 500 index put-writing strategy. Funds show 6% to 10% annual pre-fee alpha against linear factor models (1996 to 2012), yet are not reliably distinguishable from put writing; the working-paper version found the fee that would equate the put strategy to the index was about 3.6% per year, i.e. many investors did not earn their proper cost of capital. CHECK: for any strategy with short-volatility or crash exposure (credit, merger arb, levered carry, "market neutral"), benchmark against a levered index put-writing series, not a linear beta. [NEW] (Jurek and Stafford, "The Cost of Capital for Alternative Investments," Journal of Finance 2015, NBER w19643, https://www.nber.org/papers/w19643)

## 0 Master Playbook

### 0.4 Fraud and verification (additions)

- INCOME OVERSTATEMENT IS SYSTEMATIC IN BOOMS. Mian (Princeton) and Sufi measured mortgage-application income growth against IRS income growth by zip code, 2002 to 2005. In low-credit-score zip codes the normal positive correlation between the two broke down in the non-GSE market; in the 18 zip codes Interthinx rated most fraud-plagued, application income was about 90% above average IRS income. Overstating zip codes later had the highest defaults, falling income and rising poverty. CHECK: never underwrite borrower- or tenant-stated income; verify against IRS transcripts (Form 4506-C), payroll records or bank deposits, and test the pool against an external income benchmark (IRS SOI zip-code income, BLS wages). A stated-income pool whose income growth outruns local IRS income growth is a fraud signal, not a credit improvement. [NEW] (Mian and Sufi, "Fraudulent Income Overstatement on Mortgage Applications During the Credit Expansion of 2002 to 2005," RFS 2017, NBER w20947, https://www.nber.org/papers/w20947; Princeton SPIA summary, https://spia.princeton.edu/news/how-income-fraud-made-housing-bubble-worse)

### 0.6 Valuation (additions)

- MANAGEMENT FORECAST RANGES ARE TOO NARROW. Ben-David, Graham and Harvey collected over 13,300 CFO probability forecasts: realized market returns fell inside executives' stated 80% confidence intervals only 36% of the time. Miscalibrated executives were also miscalibrated about their own projects, and their firms invested more and borrowed more. CHECK: treat a management "downside case" as a midpoint, not a floor. Widen sponsor ranges roughly 2x before stress testing, and build the downside yourself from historical outcomes (base rates), not from the sponsor's scenario tab. [NEW] (Ben-David, Graham and Harvey, "Managerial Miscalibration," QJE 2013, NBER w16215, https://www.nber.org/papers/w16215)

## 6 Energy and Infrastructure (continued)

### 6.5 The numbers (additions)

- DATA-CENTER LOAD CONTEXT. US data-center electricity use rose from 76 TWh (1.9% of US demand) in 2018 to more than 200 TWh (4.8%) by 2025; utilities in the Midwest, Southeast, Southwest and Texas expect data centers to raise peak demand 20% to 40% or more over a decade. ERCOT forecast 33 GW of new data-center and crypto-mining demand by 2031 (36% of its 91 GW all-time peak) and has floated peak demand reaching 175 GW by 2032. Campus sizes are heavily right-skewed: most Texas campuses are under 0.5 GW, a few are multi-GW. [NEW] (Crosier, Onghai and Sircar (Princeton ORFE / Bendheim / Andlinger), "AI for AI: Optimizing Additional Infrastructure Build-out to Power AI Data Centers," arXiv 2609.08166, Sept 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08166)
- MERCHANT GENERATION INTO DATA-CENTER DEMAND: THE CANNIBALIZATION TRAP. The same Princeton model shows that because each new plant lowers the clearing price earned on the investor's entire fleet, optimal build intensity falls toward zero as capacity accumulates, even while load keeps arriving; uncertainty in load forecasts and execution risk weakens incentives further. Under ERCOT calibration, with no supply response the average wholesale price roughly doubles in a decade; with optimal investment it rises from about $30 to about $46-49/MWh over six years, with a wide distribution of outcomes. CHECK: a merchant plant underwritten on "AI demand" must show the price deck with other announced capacity in the same zone included (the "everyone builds" case), not just its own MW added to today's stack. Point forecasts of load are not acceptable; ask for a distribution. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 6.8 "merchant project with contracted-level DSCR"] (same source)
- Retrospective studies saying load growth lowered retail rates (LBNL/Brattle 2019-2024; EPRI/Watershed 2015-2024) were measured in a period of spare capacity; they say little about wholesale energy and capacity prices once supply is tight. Do not let a sponsor use them to dismiss ratepayer or political backlash risk (a 20% residential bill increase in parts of New Jersey became a 2025 governor's-race issue). [NEW] (same source)
- VALUING A MERCHANT GAS PLANT: a gas-fired plant is economically a strip of spark-spread call options (power price minus heat rate x gas price, minus variable O&M and, where applicable, carbon cost), one per dispatch hour across its life. Princeton ORFE (Carmona and Coulon) give a structural model linking power prices to fuel prices and capacity. CHECK: for any partly merchant plant, the IE or market advisor must deliver spark-spread distributions, not a single forward curve; DSCR is tested at the 10th-percentile spark spread, and the model must reproduce the plant's historical capacity factor before it is trusted. Panda Temple (Chapter 6.7) failed on exactly this variable. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 6.7] (Carmona and Coulon, "Electricity price modeling and asset valuation: a multi-fuel structural approach," https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1205.2299; Carmona, Coulon and Schwarz, "The Valuation of Clean Spread Options," https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1205.2302)
- POLICY-RISK SIZING (OBBBA, July 2025). Princeton REPEAT estimated the 2025 reconciliation law (One Big Beautiful Bill Act), by largely repealing IRA clean-electricity credits, cuts US additions by about 29 GW of solar and 43 GW of wind through 2030 (about 140 GW and 160 GW through 2035), cuts cumulative electricity and clean-fuels investment by about $0.5T over 2025-2035, and raises household energy costs about $165/yr in 2030 and $280/yr in 2035. Results are approximate and not peer reviewed. CHECK: any renewables deal whose returns depend on 45Y/48E/45X credits or transferability must have tax counsel confirm the begin-construction and placed-in-service deadlines, FEOC (foreign-entity) restrictions and safe-harbor evidence (physical work or 5% spend) under current Treasury guidance, plus a no-credit downside case. [NEW] (REPEAT Project, "Impacts of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act," Zenodo, July 2025, https://zenodo.org/records/15801701)

### 6.8 Red flags (additions)
- A demand case built from a single national or ISO load forecast with no distribution and no competing-supply case. [NEW]
- Pre-FID development dollars priced as if they were construction or operating dollars. [NEW]
- A "fast-track" data-center interconnection with no written non-firm or flexible service terms and no curtailment study. [NEW]

### 6.9 Hard questions (additions)
- How much of your capital is pre-FID, how much pre-COD, and what is the maximum cumulative cash at risk before first revenue? [NEW]
- How many MW of other generation are announced or in the queue in your zone, and what happens to your capture price if half of it is built? [NEW]

## 11 Crypto and digital assets

### 11.1 Custody, exchanges and proof of reserves (additions)

- BASE RATE OF EXCHANGE FAILURE. The Princeton Bitcoin textbook classifies exchange risk into three types, all of which have occurred: a bank run (fractional reserves, simultaneous withdrawals), an operator fraud or Ponzi, and a hack (including insiders). A 2013 study (Moore and Christin) found 18 of 40 Bitcoin exchanges had closed through failure or inability to pay customers. Mt. Gox, once the largest exchange, became insolvent without being able to explain where the coins went. [NEW] (Narayanan, Bonneau, Felten, Miller and Goldfeder, "Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies," Princeton University Press, free pre-publication draft, ch. 4.4, https://bitcoinbook.cs.princeton.edu)
- PROOF OF RESERVES IS HALF A PROOF. A proof of reserves (payment-to-self plus signing a third-party challenge string with the same key) proves only that whoever controls those coins cooperated, and only a lower bound on assets. Solvency needs a matching proof of liabilities: a Merkle sum tree with every customer balance as a leaf, a signed root, and each customer able to verify inclusion and that every node sums correctly with no negative balances. Reserves alone can be borrowed for the snapshot. CHECK: (1) require both reserves and liabilities proofs, dated the same block; (2) the challenge string comes from you or an independent party, not the exchange; (3) a sample of real customer accounts independently verifies inclusion; (4) check that the reserve addresses were not funded shortly before the snapshot and drained after (on-chain flow review around the attestation date); (5) liabilities include derivatives, loans and fiat, not just spot crypto. FTX had no meaningful proof of liabilities; the bankruptcy estate later found customer and corporate funds were commingled. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 11.1 "independent custody"] (same source, ch. 4.4 "Proof of reserve" and "Proof of liabilities"; FTX debtors' first interim report, summarized by Axios, https://www.axios.com/2023/06/27/ftx-management-misuse-customer-funds)
- COLD STORAGE AND KEY CONTROL. Hot and cold wallets must use separate keys; cold keys should be split (threshold secret sharing or t-of-n multisignature) so no single person can move funds. CHECK: obtain the key-ceremony documentation, the signer list and geographic separation, and test that withdrawals require the stated quorum. A single founder holding keys is the crypto version of the 0.4 segregation-of-duties failure (QuadrigaCX, 2019: after the CEO died, reportedly the sole key holder, the Ontario Securities Commission found the exchange had run as a Ponzi, with fictitious balances credited to aliases and at least C$169M of client losses). [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 0.4] (same source, ch. 4.2-4.3; OSC staff report "QuadrigaCX: A Review," June 2020, https://osc.ca/quadrigacxreport/index.html)

### 11.1 Token economics and bubble dynamics (additions)

- TOKEN NETWORKS CAN BREAK DOWN BY DESIGN. Sockin and Xiong (Princeton) model a token as membership in a platform: because user participation and token price feed each other, the token market can collapse; speculator sentiment crowds out real users and is the source of fragility; expected miner/validator attacks worsen it. CHECK: separate holders and volume into actual platform users versus speculators (wallet-level activity: fees paid for use versus transfers between exchanges). A token whose volume is mostly exchange-to-exchange churn has no user floor. [NEW] (Sockin and Xiong, "A Model of Cryptocurrencies," Management Science 2023, NBER w26816, https://www.nber.org/papers/w26816)
- SUPPLY UNLOCKS PUNCTURE BUBBLES. Xiong's review of historical bubbles (tulips, South Sea, railways, 1920s, internet, Chinese warrants) finds four common features: they coincide with technological or financial innovation; they feature frenzied trading, often by new investors; they are vulnerable to increases in asset supply; and they can burst without warning. The internet bubble's collapse coincided with mass IPO lockup expirations in early 2000 (Ofek and Richardson). In the 2005-2008 Chinese warrant bubble, warrants with near-zero fundamental value turned over 328% a day on average. CHECK: for any token or newly listed asset, map the full unlock and emission schedule (team, investors, treasury, staking rewards) against current float and average daily volume; an unlock above several days of real volume within the hold period is a structural price risk. Turnover far above fundamentals is a bubble signature, not a liquidity strength. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 11.1 "token distribution and lockups"] (Xiong, "Bubbles, Crises, and Heterogeneous Beliefs," NBER w18905, https://www.nber.org/papers/w18905)
- Rational speculators ride bubbles rather than correct them. Brunnermeier and Nagel showed hedge funds were heavily overweight tech stocks in 1998-2000, cut exposure before the peak, and the large fund that refused to ride (Tiger) did not survive. Implication for diligence: a sophisticated co-investor being in a hot asset is not evidence of value; ask what their exit plan and timing assumption are. [NEW] (Brunnermeier and Nagel, "Hedge Funds and the Technology Bubble," Journal of Finance 2004, https://www.princeton.edu/~markus/research/papers/hedgefunds_bubble.htm)

## 0 Master Playbook (continued)

### 0.7 IC memo skeleton: add a bias audit section

- ADD STEP 7A, "BIAS AUDIT," using the Kahneman-Lovallo-Sibony 12-question checklist (Kahneman was Princeton's Eugene Higgins Professor of Psychology). The deal team answers it in writing; the IC chair reads it before the vote. (1) Self-interest: does the team's comp or pipeline target skew the call? (2) Affect: is the team in love with the deal? (3) Groupthink: were dissenting views recorded and explored? (4) Saliency: is the thesis an analogy to one memorable past win? (5) Confirmation: were credible alternatives (pass, smaller ticket, different structure) evaluated? (6) Availability: what would we want to know in a year, and can we get it now? (7) Anchoring: where did each key number come from; is it the sponsor's figure re-labeled? (8) Halo: are we assuming the sponsor's success in one asset class carries to this one? (9) Sunk cost: are we attached because of time already spent or a prior deal with this sponsor? (10) Is the base case too optimistic (planning fallacy, competitor neglect)? (11) Is the worst case bad enough (disaster neglect)? (12) Is the team being too cautious? [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 0.7, A.3 red-team prompt] (Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony, "Before You Make That Big Decision," HBR June 2011, summarized at https://fs.blog/before-you-make-that-big-decision; course copy https://faculty.washington.edu/pathaksd/BBUS549/Required%20Readings/Before%20You%20Make%20that%20Big%20Decision.pdf)
- PAIR WITH THE MISCALIBRATION FINDING (0.6 above): executives' 80% ranges captured outcomes only 36% of the time. The checklist's questions 10 and 11 should be answered with an outside-view base rate (reference class: similar deals, their actual outcome distribution), not with the sponsor's scenarios. [NEW] (Ben-David, Graham and Harvey, QJE 2013, https://www.nber.org/papers/w16215)

## NEW: 0.10 Credit-cycle and bubble overlay (proposed new Master Playbook section)

Every deal is underwritten at a point in the credit cycle. This section adds a macro screen run before the IC vote.

- HOUSEHOLD-DEBT BOOMS PREDICT BUSTS. Across 30 countries, 1960-2012, a one-standard-deviation rise in household debt to GDP over three years (6.2 percentage points) predicts 2.1 percentage points lower GDP growth over the following three years, plus higher unemployment; IMF and OECD forecasters systematically over-predicted growth at the end of these booms. Household debt predicts downturns better than corporate debt, and the booms are driven by credit supply (falling spreads, rising share of risky issuance), not by income growth. CHECK: for consumer credit, consumer brands, housing, and any deal whose exit depends on consumer demand, record the 3-year change in household debt to GDP (BIS / Fed Z.1) for the relevant market, and run a downside case with revenue growth 2 points lower for 3 years if the change is in the top third of history. [NEW] (Mian, Sufi and Verner, "Household Debt and Business Cycles Worldwide," QJE 2017, NBER w21581, https://www.nber.org/papers/w21581)
- CHEAP CREDIT, NOT BROAD OPTIMISM, INFLATES PRICES. Mian and Sufi (research funded by Princeton's Julis-Rabinowitz Center) show that the 2003 surge in private-label securitization expanded mortgage credit through lenders funded by non-core deposits; zip codes more exposed saw transaction volume surge, driven almost entirely by a small group of speculators (repeat buyers, under about 1.5% of the population), followed by an amplified boom and bust. Easy credit lets a small optimistic minority set the marginal price. CHECK: in a real estate or asset-backed deal, ask what share of recent comparable sales were to investors or repeat buyers and how those buyers were financed; if comps are set by levered investors financed by a lender class that has since pulled back (e.g. non-bank or private-label), the comp is not a durable value. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 1.5] (Mian and Sufi, "Credit Supply and Housing Speculation," RFS 2022, NBER w24823, https://www.nber.org/papers/w24823)
- BUBBLE CHECKLIST (four common features, Xiong): (1) a technological or financial innovation story; (2) frenzied trading and an influx of new, inexperienced investors; (3) a coming increase in supply (new issuance, IPOs, token unlocks, new construction); (4) sudden bursts without a visible trigger. CHECK: if three of the four are present in the asset class at entry, the IC memo must state the exit assumption explicitly in terms of who the buyer will be and at what multiple once supply arrives. [NEW] (Xiong, NBER w18905, https://www.nber.org/papers/w18905)
- BUBBLES FINANCED BY BANKS ARE THE DANGEROUS ONES. Using about 30 years of bank-level data, Brunnermeier, Rother and Schnabel find bank systemic risk rises during a bubble's build-up and rises further in the bust, more so for larger banks and banks with high loan growth; after a median real estate bust, systemic risk of banks with unfavorable characteristics rises by almost 70% of the median. CHECK: when a borrower or fund relies on bank or warehouse facilities concentrated in an asset class that has boomed, stress the facility's renewal (advance-rate cuts, non-renewal) at the same time as the asset-price decline: the lender will be under stress when you need it. [NEW] (Brunnermeier, Rother and Schnabel, "Asset Price Bubbles and Systemic Risk," RFS 2020, NBER w25775, https://nber.org/papers/w25775)
- LIQUIDITY SPIRALS. Brunnermeier and Pedersen show that market liquidity and funding liquidity reinforce each other: falling prices raise margins and haircuts (margin spiral) and inflict losses on levered holders (loss spiral), so liquidity can vanish suddenly and across assets at once. CHECK: for any levered vehicle (NAV facility, margin loan, repo-funded credit fund, crypto lender), obtain the haircut/margin schedule and model a simultaneous 20-30% collateral price fall plus a haircut increase; confirm what assets are sold first and whether a forced sale would breach covenants elsewhere. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 4.2, 10.4 "subscription-line usage"] (Brunnermeier and Pedersen, "Market Liquidity and Funding Liquidity," RFS 2009, https://www.princeton.edu/~markus/research/papers/Mkt_Fun_Liquidity.pdf)

## 1 Real Estate

### 1.4 Diligence checklist (additions)

- FORECLOSURE REGIME IS A COLLATERAL VARIABLE. In 21 US states a lender must sue in court before a foreclosure sale (judicial foreclosure). Non-judicial states were about twice as likely to foreclose on delinquent owners, and comparing zip codes across state borders, foreclosure propensity and housing inventory jump as you cross into non-judicial states; moving from median to 90th-percentile foreclosure intensity was associated with about 9% lower house price growth (2007-2009). CHECK: model time-to-recovery and carry by state (judicial states run materially longer, with legal cost and property deterioration during the delay), and model local price pressure from concentrated foreclosures when the collateral pool is geographically clustered. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 1.7 legal and structure] (Mian, Sufi and Trebbi, "Foreclosures, House Prices, and the Real Economy," Journal of Finance 2015, NBER w16685, https://www.nber.org/papers/w16685)
- INCOME VERIFICATION FOR RESIDENTIAL CREDIT, DSCR AND SINGLE-FAMILY RENTAL DEALS: see 0.4 above (Mian and Sufi). Stated income in boom periods in low-credit-score areas ran up to 90% above IRS-reported income. CHECK: IRS transcripts or bank-deposit verification on a statistically meaningful sample of the tape; compare pool income growth to local IRS SOI income growth. [NEW]

### 1.8 Failure modes (additions)

- CASE FILE: 2003-2006 private-label mortgage boom. Credit supply expansion through non-core-deposit lenders, a small group of speculators setting marginal prices, income overstatement on applications, and the subsequent bust. THE CHECK: who set the comps and how were they financed; independent income verification; foreclosure-regime-adjusted recovery timing. [NEW] (Mian and Sufi papers above)

## 4 Credit of All Kinds

### 4.2 / NEW sub-strategy row: insurance-linked balance sheets and PE-owned insurers

- SHADOW REINSURANCE HIDES LEVERAGE. Koijen and Yogo (Yogo at Princeton) found life insurers ceded liabilities to affiliated, less-regulated "shadow" reinsurers (captives, often offshore), growing from about $11B (2002) to $370B (2013); by 2012 about 28 cents of every dollar insured was ceded to shadow reinsurers, up from 2 cents in 2002. Adjusting for it cut the average company's risk-based capital by about 53 percentage points and implied ratings about 3 notches lower, with expected impairment probability rising severalfold. Industry critics (Harrington for ACLI) argue the effect is overstated. CHECK: when the counterparty, lender or LP is an insurer (or an asset manager whose permanent capital is an affiliated insurer), obtain statutory statements (not GAAP) of the operating company and its affiliated reinsurers, Schedule S ceded reinsurance, and recompute RBC on a consolidated basis including affiliated captives. [NEW] (Koijen and Yogo, "Shadow Insurance," Econometrica 2016, NBER w19568, https://www.nber.org/papers/w19568; critique: https://www.acli.com/-/media/acli/public/files/pdfs-public-site/public-public-policy/captives/captive_reinsurance_in_life_ins_harrington_2014.pdf)
- RELATED DATA POINT: life-insurer private-placement lending reached about $849B (14% of balance sheets) in 2024, with PE-owned life insurers the main driver; their private placements in ABS and financial borrowers rose from 2% to 8% of assets, 2017-2024. Relevant when a private-credit manager's "permanent capital" is an affiliated insurer: the manager is lending the policyholders' money into its own platform's deals. CHECK: affiliated-transaction policy, independent valuation of affiliated private placements, and NAIC designation source (private letter ratings). [NEW, non-Princeton context] (Meisenzahl, Overpeck and Polacek, Chicago Fed WP 2025-09, https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2025/2025-09)

### 4.3 The numbers (additions)
- Hedge and credit strategies with short-volatility payoffs should be benchmarked against levered index put-writing; see 10.5 (Jurek and Stafford). A strategy earning a steady spread with rare large losses is selling insurance, and its "alpha" in calm years is the premium. [NEW]

## 8 Cannabis and Hemp

### 8.x Valuation and exit (additions)
- THE NEGLECTED-STOCK DISCOUNT IS STRUCTURAL. Hong (Princeton at the time) and Kacperczyk found "sin" stocks (alcohol, tobacco, gaming) have lower institutional ownership and fewer analysts and earn about 2.5% a year higher risk-adjusted returns than comparables, because norm-constrained investors (pensions, endowments, many banks) avoid them and litigation risk is higher. Later work questions the size of the premium. Implication for cannabis: a smaller pool of eligible buyers and lenders means a higher cost of capital and lower exit multiples than for a comparable consumer company, and that persists until institutional constraints lift. CHECK: the exit case names realistic buyers who are actually permitted to own the asset (federal-illegality constraints on US-listed acquirers, bank lenders and exchanges) and applies a discount to consumer-staples comps rather than borrowing their multiples. [NEW] (Hong and Kacperczyk, "The Price of Sin: The Effects of Social Norms on Markets," JFE 2009, https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/sternfin/mkacperc/public_html/sin.pdf; NYU Stern summary, https://www.stern.nyu.edu/experience-stern/news/uat_024862)

## 2 Tech and Venture / 9 Growth Equity

### 2.7 Failure modes / 9.4 Red flags (additions)
- THEMATIC LAUNCHES BUY AT THE TOP. Ben-David, Franzoni, Kim and Moussawi found specialized (thematic) ETFs lose about 30% in risk-adjusted terms over their first five years, not because of fees but because they are launched holding stocks that are already overvalued after a run of performance, media attention and sentiment. The same logic applies to private "theme" vehicles (AI, crypto, space, climate SPVs) raised after a hype cycle. ## 10 Funds and Co-Investments (continued)

### 10.4 Diligence checklist: lessons from PRINCO (Princeton University Investment Company)

- MANAGER SELECTION PRINCIPLES from a former 15-year Princeton endowment staffer: (1) "Fewer, better, stronger": after the GFC Princeton cut the manager roster because more managers diluted attention without adding safety; (2) "Who matters more than when": commitment pacing follows manager quality, with a multi-year moving average only loosely tracking fundraising cycles; (3) prefer managers who create value through operations and company-building over leverage, multiple expansion or momentum; (4) look for managers who discuss portfolio problems and team issues openly, and stay engaged in bad periods. Red flags named: over-diversified rosters masking low conviction, returns driven mainly by financial engineering, momentum mistaken for skill, late-stage venture managers riding momentum, and opacity about problems. CHECK: in the track-record review, decompose each realized deal into revenue/EBITDA growth versus leverage versus multiple change (as 0.7 item 6 already requires for single deals) and score the manager on the operating share. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 10.4, 0.7] ("Four Mantras from the Endowment: A Personal Journey," Boston University Insights, https://insights.bu.edu/four-mantras-from-the-endowment-a-personal-journey/; PRINCO Investment Strategy, https://princo.princeton.edu/about/investment-strategy)
- PRINCO STRUCTURE: staff can hire and terminate managers and shift assets without board approval, which PRINCO cites as an advantage over peers. Use as a benchmark question for any fund-of-funds or OCIO: who can fire a manager, and how fast? [NEW] (PRINCO Organizational Model, https://princo.princeton.edu/about/organizational-model)
- PRIVATE-EQUITY CROWDING WARNING FROM A TOP ENDOWMENT. Princeton cut its long-term endowment return assumption from 10.2% to 8% (early 2026), with the president citing excess capital chasing limited opportunities and the loss of the special access elite endowments once had; private equity was more than two-fifths of the portfolio (about $14.7B, about 40%, at June 2025; alternatives about 78% of managed investments in 2024). The 20-year rolling return fell from over 14% (2005) to under 10% (2025). Use as an outside-view anchor: if the best-resourced LPs now assume about 8% total portfolio returns, a sponsor projecting 20%+ net IRRs for a generic buyout strategy needs specific evidence. [NEW] (iTiger/Deep News summary, https://www.itiger.com/news/1145037528; AI-CIO, https://www.ai-cio.com/?p=107176; Daily Princetonian, https://www.dailyprincetonian.com/article/2025/10/princeton-news-endowment-returns-market-private-equity-tax-research-funding-alternative-assets; PAW, https://paw.princeton.edu/inbox/princos-private-equity-investments)

## 0.3 KYC, AML and sanctions (additions: crypto-sourced wealth)

- CRYPTO SOURCE OF WEALTH NEEDS ON-CHAIN TRACING, AND THE TOOLS ARE HEURISTIC. Blockchain analytics cluster addresses using heuristics (inputs spent together share an owner; fresh change addresses), then label clusters by transacting with known services. These heuristics produce false positives (researchers in the Princeton text had to retract a publicized link caused by an online wallet commingling coins), and "taint" scores assume the adversary uses the same mechanical method, so a low taint score is not proof of clean funds. Mixing services and CoinJoin are designed to defeat these heuristics; custodial wallets mix by default but keep internal records. CHECK: for any investor or counterparty whose wealth or funds derive from crypto: (1) obtain wallet addresses and a signed message proving control; (2) commission a tracing report from an analytics vendor covering the full history to fiat on-ramps, with mixer, darknet, sanctioned-address (OFAC SDN lists crypto addresses) and high-risk-exchange exposure; (3) obtain exchange account statements and KYC records for the on-ramp; (4) treat any mixer or privacy-protocol exposure as an escalation, not an automatic pass or fail, and document the judgment. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 0.3 "source of funds AND source of wealth"] (Narayanan et al., "Bitcoin and Cryptocurrency Technologies," Princeton University Press, ch. 6 "Bitcoin and Anonymity," free draft at https://bitcoinbook.cs.princeton.edu)
- North Korean state hackers (Lazarus Group) are a primary source of stolen crypto laundered through mixers and DeFi; a Princeton fellowship policy paper documents ransomware and exchange hacks as a sanctions-evasion revenue source. CHECK: OFAC screening for crypto counterparties must include address-level screening, not only name screening. [NEW] (June Park, Princeton Fung Global Fellow, "Cyber-hacking as a Means of Self-reliance," NCNK/Wilson Center, https://ncnk.org/resources/briefing-papers/all-briefing-papers/cyber-hacking-means-self-reliance)

## 2 Tech and Venture (additions)

### 2.4 The numbers
- VENTURE RETURNS ARE A RIGHT-TAIL BUSINESS. Ouyang (Princeton), Yu and Jagannathan built the aggregate portfolio of all equity investments in 17,242 US ventures first funded 1980-2006, tracked to 2018 or exit: IRR about 22% and generalized PME 1.44, but dropping the best 5% of ventures makes the risk-adjusted return negative, and returns decline after 1999. They also confirm that later-stage headline (post-money) valuations are biased upward (Gornall and Strebulaev), which distorts round-to-exit return math but not cash-on-cash. CHECK: (1) a VC manager's track record must be shown with and without its top one or two outcomes; (2) a fund thesis that relies on "consistent singles" contradicts the base rate; (3) never mark or price off the headline post-money of a round with liquidation preferences, ratchets or participation; value the specific security. [NEW] [STRENGTHENS 2.4, 10.4] (Ouyang, Yu and Jagannathan, "Return to Venture Capital in the Aggregate," NBER w27690, https://www.nber.org/papers/w27690)

## 5 Private Equity and M&A / 7 CPG (additions)

### 5.3 Legal and HR workstream
- NO-POACH CLAUSES ARE A LIABILITY, NOT A MOAT. Krueger (Princeton) and Ashenfelter found about 58% of major franchisors' 2016 franchise agreements (FDDs) contained no-poaching-of-workers clauses (examples: McDonald's, Burger King, Jiffy Lube, H&R Block), most common in low-wage, high-turnover sectors. Following the paper, the Washington State Attorney General and others forced their abandonment across many chains; antitrust class actions followed. CHECK: in any franchise, multi-unit, staffing or roll-up target, review franchise agreements, FDD Item 17, employment agreements and vendor contracts for no-poach, non-solicit and wage-fixing provisions; confirm removal and any open AG or class-action exposure; confirm non-compete enforceability by state for key employees. [NEW] (Krueger and Ashenfelter, "Theory and Evidence on Employer Collusion in the Franchise Sector," NBER w24831 / Journal of Human Resources 2022, https://www.nber.org/papers/w24831)
