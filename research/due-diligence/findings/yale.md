## 0 The Master Playbook

### 0.1 How top firms run a diligence process

- **Sequence effects distort committee and credit decisions.** Run the IC and credit approvals so a decision does not depend on what came before it. In a field experiment with Indian loan officers paid a flat fee per approval, officers were 8 percentage points less likely to approve an application if they had just approved the previous one. US asylum judges were 3.3 points more likely to deny after a grant. The bias shrank when pay rewarded accuracy, and with experience. CHECK: score deals against fixed written criteria before discussion, randomize review order when batching, and pay reviewers for accuracy, not volume. [NEW] (Chen, Moskowitz and Shue, "Decision-Making under the Gambler's Fallacy," QJE 2016; NBER Digest summary, https://www.nber.org/digest/jun16/gamblers-fallacy ; NBER w22026, https://www.nber.org/papers/w22026)
- **Polish in a pitch is a known bias.** Machine scoring of more than 1,100 accelerator pitch videos found that teams with an above-average "pitch factor" (visual, vocal and verbal enthusiasm) were about 35% more likely to be funded. Those funded teams then did worse on follow-on financing and survival. For all-women teams, decisions were seven times more sensitive to pitch delivery. CHECK: score the written materials and data room before the live pitch, use a fixed rubric, and use more than one rater. [NEW] (Song Ma and Allen Hu, "Human Interactions and Financial Investment: A Video-Based Approach," via Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/passionate-startup-pitch-is-powerful-but-can-be-misleading)
- **Go around the table, then run a post-mortem on every loss.** Blackstone's practice as Schwarzman described it at Yale: every deal team member is polled at committee. After an early failure the firm changed its decision process firm-wide. After losses it reviews which variables were misjudged and which outcomes were bad luck. Even then he estimated only "90 to 93%" of decisions were right. Add a formal post-mortem step to 0.1 step 7 (monitoring). [STRENGTHENS 0.1] (Stephen Schwarzman guest lecture, Yale ECON 252 Financial Markets 2008, lecture 20, https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-08/lecture-20)
- **Jargon test before any vote.** Opaque terms let directors and committee members approve what they do not understand. Sonnenfeld cites JPMorgan's 2012 "London Whale" losses (more than $6B, about $1B in fines), which investigations traced to governance confusion over a strategy described as "largely gibberish." CHECK: for every key term in the memo ("compute," "visibility," "risk-off," "synthetic," "hedge"), make the sponsor restate it in one plain sentence with a number attached. If no one in the approval chain can do that, the item is not approved. [NEW] (Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, "How Corporate Jargon Obscures the Truth and Fuels Disaster," Yale Insights, Sept 2025, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/how-corporate-jargon-obscures-the-truth-and-fuels-disaster ; YPFS London Whale collection incl. Senate PSI report, https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/ypfs-jpm-chase-london-whale/)
- **A risk framework on paper is not a control.** Lehman had a framework its regulator considered compliant. From late 2006 it exceeded risk limits, raised limits to fit positions, kept risk staff out of key decisions, and in October 2007 replaced its chief risk officer with a dealmaker. CHECK: ask for the limit-breach log and the record of limit changes for the last 24 months. Ask who the CRO reports to and whether the CRO has been replaced recently. [NEW] (YPFS, "The Lehman Brothers Bankruptcy B: Risk Limits and Stress Tests," JFC 1(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol1/iss1/3/)

### 0.2 Sponsor and management background checks

- **Self-disclosure is unreliable, so the background check carries the weight.** Brown, Goetzmann, Liang and Schwarz studied 444 operational due-diligence reports from a major hedge fund DD firm (NBER w15529; JFE 2012):
  - 41% of funds had some past legal or regulatory problem, more than twice the rate in Form ADV filings. 32% had been defendants in legal disputes and 15% had regulatory problems.
  - 21% misrepresented their past legal or regulatory problems. 28% made other statements that were wrong or could not be verified. 42% had at least one misrepresentation or inconsistency.
  - 6% disclosed some problems but hid others ("strategic misstatement"). 9% disclosed nothing and had problems.
  - 10% would have passed as clean on the DDQ and voluntary disclosure, and were caught only by the independent background check.
  - Not using a Big 4 auditor and using internal pricing were both significantly associated with misrepresentation.

  [STRENGTHENS 0.2, 10.3] (Brown, Goetzmann, Liang, Schwarz, "Trust and Delegation," NBER w15529, https://www.nber.org/papers/w15529 ; Yale ICF/RePEc amz2545)
- **Check verbal answers against documents.** In the same study, 20% of managers gave verbal figures in interviews (assets, performance) that contradicted their own written documents. CHECK: write down every number given in management meetings and reconcile it to the data room. Treat a gap as a finding, not a slip. [NEW] (Trust and Delegation, NBER w15529)
- **Refusal rate is a signal.** In 14% of investigations the fund or its administrator refused to answer some DD questions. Make "refused or deflected question" a tracked field in the A.2 diligence report. [STRENGTHENS A.2, 10.7] (Trust and Delegation, NBER w15529)
- **Form ADV is predictive.** Using the 2006 SEC registration filings, past legal and regulatory problems in Form ADV were strongly related to conflicts of interest, ownership concentration and leverage. The authors' "omega score" estimates operational risk from public data (performance, volatility, size, age, fees) when an ADV is not available. CHECK: pull Form ADV Parts 1 and 2 (IAPD) for every adviser sponsor and reconcile the disclosure items to the sponsor's own answers. [STRENGTHENS 0.2] (Brown, Goetzmann, Liang, Schwarz, "Mandatory Disclosure and Operational Risk," JF 2008, RePEc ysm amz2472; "Estimating Operational Risk for Hedge Funds: The omega-Score," FAJ 2009; abstract-level, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:63:y:2008:i:6:p:2785-2815)
- **Fraud is usually a team activity, so diligence the team as well as the principal.** Public firms that could move to remote work after 2020 saw misconduct fall about 2 percentage points more, roughly half the baseline rate. The drop was nearly twice as large at firms with strong in-person team cultures. The authors' reading is that remote work made collusion harder to sustain. CHECK: run background checks on the CFO, controller and the person who releases wires, not only the named principal. Look for long-tenured, closed inner circles that control both reporting and cash. [NEW] (John Barrios, Guo and Zhu, via Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/remote-work-is-linked-to-decline-in-financial-misconduct)
- **A "CFO" who is really a controller is a gap.** Investors in small and family companies should check that management titles match the job actually done. They should also check for a CFO able to give an independent view, at least one director able to chair an audit committee, and clear revenue recognition. Sloppy numbers signal broader governance problems even where financials are unaudited. [STRENGTHENS 0.2, 9.2] (O'Dowd and McLarty, "Even Small Businesses Need Corporate Governance," Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/even-small-businesses-need-corporate-governance)

### 0.4 Fraud detection: the universal red flags

- **Low risk with steady returns is the core Ponzi tell, more than high returns.** Antle, who worked on the Madoff recovery team, puts Madoff's intake at about $65B and investor losses at about $18B. The pitch worked because it promised modest, stock-like returns with almost no downside. Anyone who believes higher expected return requires risk should be suspicious of low volatility combined with steady gains. The bible's red flag "guaranteed or fixed above-market returns" should be widened to "returns that are smooth relative to the stated strategy, at any level." [STRENGTHENS 0.4, 10.7] (Rick Antle, "Where Did Madoff's Money Go?," Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/where-did-madoffs-money-go)
- **Smoothness and ratio metrics can be manufactured.** Goetzmann, Ingersoll, Spiegel and Welch prove that the Sharpe ratio, Jensen's alpha, the information ratio, the Sortino ratio and M-squared can all be gamed, even with high transaction costs, by managers using derivatives or dynamic strategies. Only a utility-based measure is manipulation-proof. CHECK: never accept a Sharpe or Sortino figure as evidence of skill. Ask for the return distribution, option-like exposures and drawdowns, and compute a manipulation-proof measure (MPPM) from monthly returns. [NEW] (Goetzmann, Ingersoll, Spiegel, Welch, "Portfolio Performance Manipulation and Manipulation-Proof Performance Measures," RFS 2007, Yale ICF WP, https://repec.som.yale.edu/icfpub/publications/2471.pdf)
- **Quarter-end window dressing.** Lehman used "Repo 105" transactions to move up to $50B of assets off its balance sheet at quarter-ends and report lower leverage. Its auditor, Ernst & Young, knew of the practice and of impaired assets counted in the liquidity pool, and did not force disclosure. The examiner found colorable malpractice claims against EY. CHECK: request average daily or intra-quarter balances of debt, cash and assets, not just period-end figures. Flag any recurring financing that unwinds just after period-end. [NEW] (YPFS, "Lehman Brothers Bankruptcy C: Repo 105" and "D: The Role of Ernst & Young," JFC 1(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol1/iss1/4/ and /5/)
- **Off-balance-sheet vehicles and implied support.** Enron's partnerships and banks' SIVs held risk off the filed balance sheet, with contract language implying the sponsor would rescue the vehicle. CHECK: list every SPV, JV, guarantee, keepwell and "moral obligation" support. Require a consolidated look-through. [STRENGTHENS 0.4] (Robert Shiller, "Human Foibles, Fraud, Manipulation, and Regulation," Yale ECON 252 lecture 8, https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-08/lecture-8)
- **Customer data can catch revenue fraud at the supplier.** Across about 30,000 supplier-years (1976 to 2018), two signals predicted revenue fraud:
  - The gap between the supplier's sales growth and its major customers' purchase growth.
  - Sharp year-over-year jumps in those customers' inventory and accounts payable, which signal channel stuffing. That single variable carried about half the predictive power of the 11-variable Dechow model.

  CHECK: for the top 10 customers, compare the target's reported sales to each customer with the customer's own purchase, inventory or AP trend. Use customer confirmations or filings, or for private customers ask them directly. [NEW] (X. Frank Zhang, Chenchen Li, Ningzhong Li, "Using Economic Links between Firms to Detect Accounting Fraud," via Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/customer-data-can-reveal-revenue-fraud-at-supplier-firms)
- **No accounting function is an automatic pass.** At FTX there was no accounting department (the work was outsourced). The company could not produce a complete list of its own employees. Customer crypto deposits were not recorded as assets on the main entities' balance sheets. John Ray called it "a complete failure of corporate controls." CHECK: confirm that an in-house controller or CFO exists, that an employee census reconciles to payroll, and that the balance sheet records customer assets and the matching liabilities. [STRENGTHENS 0.4, 11.1] (Rick Antle, "Did Crypto Cause the FTX Collapse?," Yale Insights, Dec 2022, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/did-crypto-cause-the-ftx-collapse)
- **Classic sales-side tells.** Watch for overselling ("doubles every ten years"), steering good deals to insiders, churning for fees, adjusted earnings with no GAAP reconciliation, and pressure to act fast. [STRENGTHENS 0.4] (Shiller, ECON 252 lecture 8)

### 0.5 Verification of funds and assets

- **Asset and performance verification often fails, even in professional ODD.** Across 444 DD reports:
  - Nearly 19% of funds' asset information could not be independently verified, or conflicted with a third-party source.
  - 14% had discrepancies or verification problems with reported performance.
  - In 16% of cases the manager's account of the wire-signature process (who can release cash) did not match the administrator's.

  CHECK: obtain the signatory list and approval matrix from the bank or administrator directly. Do not take the manager's description. [STRENGTHENS 0.5, 10.3] (Trust and Delegation, NBER w15529)
- **Madoff's own rule.** The study quotes Madoff: to investigate a Ponzi, "You must do a third-party check. It's an absolute must... It's accounting 101." [STRENGTHENS 0.5] (quoted in Trust and Delegation, NBER w15529)
- **Treasury and bank-counterparty diligence is a cash-verification item.** Silicon Valley Bank had about 94% of deposits uninsured, against an FDIC limit of $250,000. By December 31, 2022, mark-to-market losses on its securities (about $17B) exceeded its $15B book equity, leaving about -$3B. All of this was in public filings. Depositors withdrew $42B (almost a quarter of deposits) on March 9, 2023. Signature lost about $10B and First Republic about $25B the next day.

  CHECK for every operating company and fund: where is the cash held, what share is above insured limits, and is there a covenant forcing a single bank? Require deposits to be spread across banks, or kept in insured sweeps or Treasury MMFs. [NEW] (Andrew Metrick, "The Failure of Silicon Valley Bank and the Panic of 2023," JEP 2024, https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2024-02/metrick-2024-the-failure-of-silicon-valley-bank-and-the-panic-of-2023.pdf ; Song Ma, "What's Next for the Startups That Banked with SVB?," https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/whats-next-for-the-startups-that-banked-with-svb)
- **"Cash equivalents" are not cash.** The Reserve Primary Fund ($62.5B) held $785M of Lehman paper and got more than $40B of redemption requests in two days. It broke the buck on September 16, 2008 and then suspended redemptions. Investors got about 50 cents within six weeks and 99 cents only after two years; the final distribution (99.1 cents) came in 2014. CHECK: look through MMF and "cash management" holdings, and model a 6-to-24-month gate on any pooled cash vehicle. [NEW] (YPFS, "United States: Reserve Primary Fund Suspension, 2008," JFC 7(2), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol7/iss2/9/)
- **Segregation of customer assets needs testing, not just a statement.** Lehman's US broker-dealer was legally required to segregate customer assets, but omnibus cash accounts and group-wide integration made the liquidation slow and messy. CHECK: confirm customer or fund assets sit in accounts titled to the fund or customer at a custodian outside the manager's group. [STRENGTHENS 0.5, 10.3] (YPFS, "Lehman Brothers Bankruptcy E: Effects on Lehman's U.S. Broker-Dealer," JFC 1(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol1/iss1/6/)

### 0.6 Valuation methods across assets

- **Margins do not expand with scale on average. Make projected operating leverage earn its place.** In 6,103 US IPO firms (53,079 firm-years, 1975 to 2016), median real sales growth was above 8% a year, yet cost-to-sales did not fall and profit-to-sales did not rise. The result held for all US public firms, firms worldwide and private European firms. Thomas: "If you are going to project efficiency, you have to have some concrete story." CHECK: any model where EBITDA margin rises because of scale needs a line-by-line cost bridge naming each efficiency. Otherwise hold margins flat in the base case. [NEW; CONTRADICTS the implicit margin-expansion assumption behind 2.4 Rule of 40 projections and 9.3] (Jacob Thomas, Aytekin Ertan, Stefan Lewellen, "Do Profit Margins Expand for High Growth Firms?," via Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/contrary-to-conventional-wisdom-margins-don-t-rise-as-company-grows)
- **Reverse valuation: solve for what must be true.** Thomas's way of testing a headline valuation: divide the price by a fully diluted share count, then ask what margin, growth and pricing power the price requires. Adding employee RSUs and options moved one implied valuation from $1.25T to about $1.7T. One estimate of understated equivalents put it above $2T. CHECK: always use a fully diluted count (options, RSUs, warrants, SAFEs, benefit-fund issuance). State the implied steady-state margin and growth, and test them against peers. [NEW, method for 0.6 table: "Reverse DCF / implied expectations"] (Jacob Thomas, "Is Anthropic Worth $1.25 Trillion?," Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/is-anthropic-worth-125-trillion)
- **Do not underwrite multiple expansion.** One Yale SOM practitioner's rule: buying at 4x and selling at 6x because markets rise "is not a version of value creation that we feel is predictable." Base cases should exit at or below the entry multiple. [STRENGTHENS 0.7 item 6, 5.1] (Adam Blumenthal, "How Does Private Equity Create Value?," Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/how-does-private-equity-create-value)
- **Evidence that multiple expansion dominates small-buyout returns.** In search-fund acquisitions later sold, enterprise value rose from about $1B to $4B in aggregate. About 80% came from EBITDA multiple expansion (6.3x at entry to 15.6x at exit) and only about 20% from EBITDA growth. Margins actually fell from 25% to 19%, with holds of about five years. Returns are back-end-loaded and depend on exit conditions. CHECK: in any ETA, search-fund or small-buyout deal, show the return with exit multiple equal to entry multiple. [NEW; STRENGTHENS 0.7 item 6] (A.J. Wasserstein and Jacob Thomas, "Do Search Fund CEOs Improve Performance?," Yale Insights, Apr 2026, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/do-search-fund-ceos-improve-performance)
- **Long-run real home prices are close to flat.** Shiller's index back to 1890 shows little long-run trend in real US home prices outside booms. Similar series for Norway and the Netherlands show the same, and real construction costs were flat or falling. Do not underwrite real appreciation into exit values. Default to inflation-only growth unless a specific supply constraint is documented. [STRENGTHENS 1.4, 1.5] (Shiller, "Real Estate Finance and Its Vulnerability to Crisis," ECON 252 lecture 12, https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-08/lecture-12)
- **Market-index marks are reasonable fair-value guides even in a panic.** Subprime index (ABX) prices in 2008 to 2009 had significant fundamental components, despite claims of irrationality. Marking to observable indices is defensible, and model-only marks that ignore them are not. [NEW, for 4 and 10 valuation policy] (JFC, "Revisiting Subprime Pricing Irrationality During the Global Financial Crisis," JFC 3(2), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol3/iss2/1/)

## 1 Real Estate

### 1.4 / 1.5 Checklist and numbers

- **Refinancing wall stress.** In late 2023 Metrick estimated office values down about 30%. Roughly two-thirds of office CRE debt needed refinancing within about four years. Many legacy loans carried coupons near 1% against market rates near 7%. About $3T of CRE sits on bank balance sheets, roughly two-thirds outside the 25 largest banks. CHECK: for any loan maturing within 48 months, re-underwrite at the current market rate. Size the refinance gap (new proceeds at the market DSCR minus the payoff), and name who funds it. [STRENGTHENS 1.4, 1.5 "RATES" stress] (Andrew Metrick, "Is Commercial Real Estate in for a Downturn... or a Crisis?," Yale Insights, Dec 2023, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/is-commercial-real-estate-in-for-downturnor-crisis)
- **Government tenancy is not credit-risk-free. It carries termination-for-policy risk.**
  - DOGE listed 793 federal leases (about 9M sq ft) for cancellation in 2025; 384 were still listed by August 2025.
  - GSA leases average 14 years, against 5 to 10 years for private tenants. Early-termination options are typically exercisable after year 5 on 90 to 120 days' notice, and were historically almost never used.
  - In D.C. buildings with listed leases, landlord NOI fell 5% and junk-rated CMBS fell about 4% more than comparable GSA-backed bonds, with spillovers to nearby properties. Barclays put CMBS tied to government-leased buildings at about $12B.

  CHECK: read every government lease for ETOs and termination-for-convenience rights, model the exercise case, and cap single-agency share of NOI. [NEW] (Cameron LaPoint and Soon Hyeok Choi, via Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/doges-lease-cancellations-are-already-hitting-the-commercial-real-estate-market)
- **Leverage cycles move lending terms, not just prices.** Geanakoplos documents average margins on mortgage securities (Ellington's book) falling to about 10% from late 2005 to 2007, then jumping above 40% in late 2007. In 2006 a AAA mortgage bond could be financed with a 1.6% haircut (about 60:1). Average leverage on the $2.5T of "toxic" mortgage securities was about 16:1, and about 1.2:1 by mid-2009. Implication: the refinance and takeout assumption in 1.10 must survive a regime where advance rates fall sharply at the moment you need them. [STRENGTHENS 1.10, 3.4] (John Geanakoplos, "The Leverage Cycle," Cowles Foundation DP 1715 / NBER Macro Annual 2009, https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/d1715.pdf)
- **Single-use and single-infrastructure dependency.** Canary Wharf succeeded only after the government linked it to the subway, and was built as office-only with no residential or retail. CHECK: identify any single transport link, anchor tenant sector or public approval the value depends on, and run the "link delayed" case. [NEW] (Yale SOM case, "Canary Wharf: Financing and Placemaking," https://cases.som.yale.edu/canary-wharf)
- **Insurability is a diligence gate.** In parts of California wildfire coverage is unavailable, and banks will not lend against uninsurable property, leaving cash-only buyers and lower values. In property-tax states, falling values erode the municipal revenue base, a possible "nasty spiral." CHECK: obtain bindable insurance quotes (not indications) for the full hold, and stress a 2x premium. Flag any asset where coverage depends on a state insurer of last resort. [NEW] (Yale Insights, "Putting a Price on Climate Risk," https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/putting-price-on-climate-risk)
- **Water certainty underpins Sun Belt development.** The 1922 Colorado River Compact allocates 16.5M acre-feet a year, but 2000 to 2023 flows averaged about 12.5M (a structural deficit of about 4M a year). Lakes Mead and Powell are at about one-third of their 2000 levels, and further flow declines of 20% to 30% are projected. CHECK: for Phoenix, Las Vegas and Denver Front Range development, confirm the will-serve letter and water source seniority (senior versus junior rights), and stress water cost at post-2026 tiered pricing. [NEW] (Todd Cort, "The Colorado River Is Overdrawn," Yale Insights, May 2026, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/the-colorado-river-is-overdrawn-and-corporate-reckoning-is-imminent)
- **Historical anchor on leverage.** In the 1920s typical home loans were 60% LTV with roughly five-year balloons. The 30-year self-amortizing loan only became standard in the 1950s. Short-balloon structures are the historical norm in crises, which is why the takeout test matters. [STRENGTHENS 1.10] (Shiller, ECON 252 lecture 12)

### 1.8 Failure modes (new case files)

- **CASE FILE: GSA-leased office and CMBS, 2025.** Leases assumed locked for 14 years were exposed to early termination. THE CHECK: ETO and termination-right review, and a single-tenant concentration cap. [NEW] (LaPoint and Choi, above)
- **CASE FILE: First Republic Bank, 2023, as a lender to real estate.** The bank had $212.6B of assets and a book of low-coupon jumbo mortgages funded by uninsured deposits. It lost $25B of deposits on March 10 and $40B more on March 13, and borrowed up to $109B from the Fed. A $30B deposit from 11 banks bought only five weeks, and the failure cost the FDIC about $15.6B. THE CHECK, for anyone whose construction or bridge loan sits with a regional lender: confirm the lender's funding mix, and that the draw commitment survives a lender failure (replacement lender, assignment rights). [NEW] (YPFS, "United States: First Republic Bank Emergency Liquidity Program, 2023," JFC 7(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol7/iss1/24/)

### 1.10 Hard questions (add)

- What is the lease-by-lease termination right schedule, and what is NOI if every government or anchor ETO is exercised on first date? [NEW]
- At today's coupon, what refinance proceeds does the asset support, and who writes the check for the gap? [NEW]
- Is the property insurable at a bindable price for the full hold, and what is the water source and its priority? [NEW]
- If your construction lender fails mid-draw, who funds the remaining commitment? [NEW]

## 2 Tech and Venture

- **VC manager dispersion is extreme, so manager selection is the main decision.** In Swensen's data, the gap between first- and third-quartile managers over ten years to June 2005 was about 0.5 points a year in fixed income and about 2 in large-cap equity. It was 13.7 in buyouts. Venture capital had a 43.2-point gap between top and bottom quartile (Swensen's VC figure is top versus bottom, not first versus third). He added that "there's absolutely no way" to index venture capital. [STRENGTHENS 2.1, 10.4] (David Swensen guest lecture, ECON 252 lecture 9, https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-08/lecture-9)
- **Top-VC persistence comes from deal access, not from better picking or advising.** Top-performing VC firms over a five-year window are highly likely to be top performers in the next five. The authors ruled out superior selection and superior advice as the main drivers; persistence came from access to later-stage, larger, syndicated deals that reputation unlocks. Statistically only one or two in ten VC investments succeed. Reputation often sits with partners rather than the firm. CHECK: attribute the track record to the partners who led each deal, and test whether those partners are still there and still leading. [STRENGTHENS 10.4] (Olav Sorenson, Ramana Nanda, Sampsa Samila, via Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/for-top-venture-capital-firms-success-breeds-success)
- **Corporate VC money is short-lived and opportunistic.** In US public firms (1980 to 2006), a drop in the parent's innovation raised the odds of launching a CVC unit by about 26%. The median CVC lasted four years (against 10 to 12 for traditional VC), and CVCs usually ended once the parent recovered. Parents often cite the startup's patents afterward, a sign of knowledge transfer. CHECK when a strategic investor is on the cap table: do not count on follow-on support. Review information rights, board observer rights and ROFR or ROFN clauses for technology-leakage and exit-blocking risk. [NEW] (Song Ma, "The Life Cycle of Corporate Venture Capital," via Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/companies-invest-in-startups-to-repair-weaknesses)
- **Pitch-delivery bias.** See 0.1 (Song Ma and Hu). Add to 2.8: "Investment case rests on founder charisma rather than data-room evidence." [STRENGTHENS 2.8]
- **Margins do not expand with scale.** See 0.6 (Thomas, Ertan, Lewellen). Add to 2.9: "Name each specific cost line that falls as a share of revenue, and why." [STRENGTHENS 2.4, 2.9]
- **Bank concentration is a portfolio-level risk.** Hundreds of startups held large uninsured balances at SVB, which threatened payroll. Add to 2.2 data room: "bank accounts list with balances by institution and insured share, and any loan covenant requiring exclusive banking." Add to 2.9: "If your bank closed this morning, how many weeks of payroll could you meet?" [NEW] (Song Ma, Yale Insights; Metrick, JEP 2024)
- **Fully diluted means everything.** See 0.6 reverse valuation. Benefit-fund issuance, RSUs and options changed one implied valuation by hundreds of billions. [STRENGTHENS 2.3 cap-table item]
- **GP economics in venture.** In Metrick and Yasuda's sample, the median VC fund's expected PV of GP revenue was $23.50 per $100 committed: about $14.61 management fees and $8.86 carry. Median lifetime fees were 17.75% of committed capital (IQR 14% to 21.25%). Only 47.6% of VC funds had a hurdle. Most VC fund revenue is fixed, not performance-based. [NEW, also 10.5] (Metrick and Yasuda, "The Economics of Private Equity Funds," Wharton WP 2007 / RFS 2010, https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/0717.pdf)

## 3 Debt to Businesses

- **Private credit opacity means forbearance can hide losses.**
  - Private lenders often hold a borrower's entire debt and need not mark or trade it, so they can forbear indefinitely. The authors warn this may create "zombie" borrowers, and market prices that once gave early warning of trouble largely disappear.
  - Assets in private-credit strategies grew from about $300B in 2010 to an estimated $1.6T in 2023.
  - Loans held by private-credit BDCs rose from 1,395 to 20,182 between 2010 and 2022. BDC filings are one of the few public windows: lender identity, rate, amount, maturity and book value for a subset of borrowers.

  CHECK: for any private credit position or fund, track amend-and-extend, PIK toggles and maturity extensions by loan. Compare the manager's mark with the same loan's mark in other BDCs' Schedules of Investments. [NEW; STRENGTHENS 4.1] (Jared Elias and Elisabeth de Fontenay, "The Credit Markets Go Dark," Yale Law Journal 134(3), https://yalelawjournal.org/index.php/article/the-credit-markets-go-dark)
- **Same-sponsor lending is a conflict to screen.** PE sponsors increasingly run private credit funds. Sponsors normally avoid lending from their own credit funds to their own portfolio companies because of the conflict. CHECK: confirm whether the lender is affiliated with the equity sponsor, and how amendments and waivers are approved. [NEW] (Elias and de Fontenay, YLJ)
- **Credit officers' sequence bias.** See 0.1: the loan-officer gambler's-fallacy result. Paying credit staff per approval amplified the bias. [NEW]
- **Borrowing-base fraud check from the customer side.** Apply the Zhang, Li and Li channel-stuffing test (0.4) to receivables pools. Large year-over-year jumps in a debtor's inventory and AP against the borrower's invoices are a red flag for pre-billing or channel stuffing. Pair with debtor verification calls. [STRENGTHENS 3.7]
- **ISDA documentation is not a resolution plan.** At Lehman's filing it had 906,000 derivative transactions under 6,120 ISDA Master Agreements ($35T notional). Derivatives' exemption from the automatic stay let counterparties terminate and novate in a rush, destroying value and spreading contagion. Local law overrode standardized terms in several jurisdictions. CHECK: for borrowers with material hedges, map the counterparties, close-out netting, and whether the swap is secured pari passu under the credit agreement. [NEW] (YPFS, "Lehman Brothers Bankruptcy F: ISDA Master Agreement" and "G: The Special Case of Derivatives," JFC 1(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol1/iss1/7/ and /8/)
- **Hard question (3.9).** "Show me every amendment, waiver, PIK election and maturity extension since close, and the mark on this loan in other lenders' books." [NEW]

## 4 Credit of All Kinds

- **Short-term secured funding runs through haircuts.** Gorton and Metrick's repo-haircut index (average haircut on nine non-Treasury collateral classes) went from about zero in early 2007 to nearly 50% at the late-2008 peak. Several collateral classes could not be repo'd at all, equivalent to a 100% haircut. Illustration: with $10T of repo and a 20% average haircut, borrowers face a $2T shortfall. CHECK for any levered credit fund, ABS warehouse or specialty lender:
  - List every financing line with its haircut or advance rate, margin-call mechanics and term.
  - Stress haircuts to crisis levels (50% for non-Treasury structured collateral, 100% for esoteric assets).
  - Confirm the unencumbered liquidity that covers the call.

  [NEW] (Gary Gorton and Andrew Metrick, "Securitized Banking and the Run on Repo," JFE 2012, Yale ICF WP, https://repec.som.yale.edu/icfpub/publications/2358.pdf)
- **Margins are procyclical.** See 1 (Geanakoplos): Ellington margins went from about 10% to above 40% in months, and AAA haircuts from 1.6% to multiples of that. Add to 4.3: "Advance rates are a state variable, not a constant. Underwrite at the stressed advance rate." [NEW]
- **Collateral reinvestment risk in "safe" lending programs.** AIG's securities-lending program reinvested cash collateral in RMBS. When borrowers returned securities, AIG could not repay the cash, and the Fed lent up to $37.8B through the Securities Borrowing Facility. Separately, CDS written on CDOs produced collateral calls that drained liquidity; Maiden Lane III ($24.3B Fed loan plus $5B AIG equity) bought the CDOs to stop the calls. CHECK: for insurers, lenders and funds, ask what the cash collateral is invested in and what collateral-posting triggers (rating downgrade, mark-to-market) exist. [NEW] (YPFS, AIG Modules B and E, JFC 3(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol3/iss1/3/ and /6/)
- **Structured credit: read the oral history.** YPFS's "Inside the CDO Machine" interviews, by a former Elliott structured-products head, and "Anatomy of a Trade: The Making of a Subprime CDO" are practitioner accounts of how ratings, warehouse risk and manager incentives failed. They suit a structured-credit appendix. [NEW, reading list] (JFC 4(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol4/iss1/10/ and /12/)
- **Bank-like lenders: the uninsured-deposit and duration test.** For any bank, credit union, fintech lender or specialty finance company that funds with deposits or short-term money:
  - Uninsured share of funding.
  - Unrealized losses on securities against equity.
  - IRRBB (economic value of equity under rate shocks).
  - An LCR-style 30-day outflow test.

  One JFC analysis argues that the Basel IRRBB standard would have flagged SVB's asset-liability strategy 10 quarters before failure, and the LCR would have forced tens of billions more liquidity at least 4 quarters before. [NEW; STRENGTHENS 11 "Financial services and specialty lenders"] (JFC, "How US Bank Regulation Failed SVB and Its Supervisors," JFC 7(2), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol7/iss2/12/ ; Metrick, JEP 2024)
- **Stress testing done right.** The JFC "Stressing the Stress Tests" recommendations translate directly to credit-fund and lender diligence:
  - Run multiple scenarios, not one.
  - Disclose stressed fair value for all securities, including HTM.
  - Stress funding and liquidity, not only credit.
  - Extend testing to smaller institutions.

  CHECK: ask the manager for HTM and AFS fair-value marks and a liquidity stress, not only a loss-rate stress. [NEW] (JFC 7(3), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol7/iss3/2/)
- **Gates and suspensions recur and carry stigma.** YPFS's survey of bank holidays and fund suspensions finds that suspensions of fund withdrawals work like bank holidays. They only pause the run and carry a stigma that makes managers reluctant to use them until late. For semi-liquid credit funds (interval funds, non-traded BDCs, evergreen vehicles), read the gate, suspension and in-kind provisions and assume they will be used. [NEW] (JFC, "Survey of Bank Holidays and Fund Suspensions," JFC 7(2), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol7/iss2/1/)

### 4.5 Failure modes (new case files)

- **CASE FILE: Silicon Valley Bank, 2023.** Uninsured deposits (94%), long securities funded by demand deposits, and a $17B unrealized loss larger than book equity, all public. A $42B one-day run. THE CHECK: uninsured-funding share, unrealized loss to equity, IRRBB and a 30-day outflow test. [NEW] (Metrick, JEP 2024)
- **CASE FILE: Credit Suisse, 2023.** Client outflows in Q4 2022 and years of scandals came first. When a major shareholder said it would add no more capital, the shares fell 24% in a day and five-year CDS went above 1,000bp. CS drew CHF 48B from standing facilities and CHF 120B from emergency ones before the UBS takeover. THE CHECK, as a counterparty, prime broker or custodian: watch CDS, deposit and AUM outflows and shareholder support. Have a documented plan to move assets and novate trades. [NEW] (YPFS, "Switzerland: Credit Suisse Emergency Liquidity Program, 2023," JFC 7(1), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol7/iss1/18/)
- **CASE FILE: Reserve Primary Fund, 2008.** See 0.5. THE CHECK: look through "cash" vehicles, and model the gate. [NEW]

## 5 Private Equity and M&A

- **Fee drag and fee structure, with numbers.** Metrick and Yasuda (238 funds, 1992 to 2006):
  - About 60% (2007 WP) to about two-thirds (2010 RFS) of expected GP revenue is fixed, not tied to performance.
  - The median buyout fund's expected PV of GP revenue is $19.36 per $100 committed: $10.34 management fees, $5.35 carry, $1.44 entry transaction fees, $2.11 monitoring and exit transaction fees.
  - Median lifetime management fees are 12% of committed capital for buyout (IQR 10% to 13.5%) and 17.75% for VC.
  - 93.1% of buyout funds had a hurdle (8% modal), mostly with a 100% catch-up.
  - About 80% of buyout LPAs share transaction fees with LPs; a third return 100%, a third split 50/50.
  - Monitoring fees run 1% to 5% of portfolio-company EBITDA a year, typically 80/20 in the LPs' favor.

  Add to 5.4 and 10.5. [NEW] (Metrick and Yasuda, Wharton WP 2007, https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/0717.pdf ; RFS 2010 version, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:23:y:2010:i:6:p:2303-2341)
- **Deal-level fee check.** In buyouts, ask for every transaction, monitoring, director and exit fee charged to the target, and the LPA offset. Monitoring agreements with acceleration clauses on exit are a red flag. [NEW] (derived from Metrick and Yasuda)
- **Buyout business scales, so does performance decay with size?** Experienced buyout GPs raise much larger funds, which raises revenue per partner while revenue per dollar falls. Persistence is weaker in buyout than in VC because of this scaling. Swensen: "Size is the enemy of performance." CHECK: compare the new fund's size with the size of the funds that earned the track record, and with deals per partner. [NEW, also 10.4] (Metrick and Yasuda RFS 2010; Metrick and Yasuda, "Venture Capital and Other Private Equity: A Survey," NBER w16652, https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16652/w16652.pdf ; Swensen, ECON 252 lecture 9)
- **Return attribution evidence.** Search funds: about 80% of value creation came from multiple expansion and about 20% from EBITDA growth, with margins falling (see 0.6). Use it as the benchmark challenge in 5.9 "Split the return." [STRENGTHENS 5.1, 5.9]
- **Owner-operator private equity has not historically beaten public equity.** Moskowitz and Vissing-Jorgensen found that entrepreneurial (owner-held) private equity returns were no higher than public equity, despite extreme concentration (about three-quarters of such holdings in a single firm). A Bank of Canada follow-up disputes how robust this is beyond the 1990s. In lower-middle-market deals, do not assume an illiquidity premium will appear on its own. [NEW; present both positions per 0.6 rule] (Tobias Moskowitz and Annette Vissing-Jorgensen, "The Returns to Entrepreneurial Investment: A Private Equity Premium Puzzle?," AER 2002, NBER w8876, https://www.nber.org/papers/w8876 ; Kartashova, Bank of Canada WP 2011-6)
- **De-SPAC and SPAC-sourced targets: net cash per share.** For SPACs that merged between January 2019 and June 2020, the cash actually delivered per $10 share averaged $4.10 (median $5.70) after redemptions, sponsor promote, warrants and fees ($7.50 before redemptions). Later deals were higher but far below $10. CHECK: for any de-SPAC, PIPE or SPAC-adjacent deal, compute net cash per share (cash minus merger costs minus warrant value, over shares). Treat headline EV as unreliable. A Harvard Forum critique disputes how much this predicts returns. [NEW] (Klausner, Ohlrogge and Ruan, "A Sober Look at SPACs," Yale Journal on Regulation 39 (2022), https://openyls.law.yale.edu/entities/publication/44dd0075-0956-44d1-bb2e-ea326755b4b5 ; Yale JREG bulletin, "Net Cash per Share: The Key to Disclosing SPAC Dilution," https://www.yalejreg.com/bulletin/net-cash-per-share-the-key-to-disclosing-spac-dilution/)
- **Antitrust: killer acquisitions cluster below HSR thresholds.** About 6% of pharma acquisitions (6.4% in the working paper) were "killer acquisitions," bought to shut down an overlapping drug project. They cluster just below Hart-Scott-Rodino notification thresholds; the authors estimate as many as 50 a year in pharma. For a buy-side or exit thesis that relies on a strategic acquirer in overlapping products, expect growing FTC and DOJ scrutiny of sub-threshold deals, and note the acquirer may shelve the asset (relevant to earn-outs). [NEW, also 11 Life sciences] (Colleen Cunningham, Florian Ederer, Song Ma, "Killer Acquisitions," JPE 2021, SSRN 3241707 ; Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/when-corporate-acquisitions-affect-healthcare)
- **Labor relations as a gating item.** One Yale SOM-affiliated sponsor walks away when it cannot reach a workable union agreement. Add "union contracts, open grievances, and pension or withdrawal liability" to 5.3 HR. [STRENGTHENS 5.3] (Blumenthal, Yale Insights)
- **Leverage norm.** Schwarzman described Blackstone's historic buyout structure as "about three dollars of debt for every one dollar of equity." The Argentina investment, funded with dollar borrowing against a currency peg, lost every dollar. CHECK: identify any currency or peg mismatch between revenue and debt. [STRENGTHENS 5.4, 5.7] (Schwarzman, ECON 252 lecture 20)

## 6 Energy and Infrastructure

- **CASE FILE: ERCOT, Winter Storm Uri, February 2021.**
  - About 30 GW of capacity was missing, against average non-summer load of about 45 GW.
  - Prices hit the $9,000/MWh cap, with some ancillary services at $25,000/MWh.
  - Losses are estimated at about $140B: about $50B in ERCOT charges and about $90B in property and casualty losses.
  - Generators had been urged to winterize after 2011 but were not paid to, and ERCOT had no enforcement power.
  - Gas compression and delivery failed, partly because rolling blackouts cut power to compressors, leaving plants without fuel.

  THE CHECK:
  - Firm fuel supply (firm transport, on-site backup fuel, and compressor power not on the curtailable list).
  - Winterization standard and the cost of meeting it.
  - Scarcity-event modelling for a merchant or hedged generator in both directions: the cost of being short a hedge at the cap, and counterparty default by retailers.

  [NEW, extends 6.7 alongside Panda Temple] (Ed Hirs interview, "Why the Texas Power Market Failed," Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/why-the-texas-power-market-failed)
- **Data center and AI-infrastructure financing is moving off balance sheet.** Hyperscaler capex was about $116B in 2022 and nearly $200B in 2024, with 2025 plans of about $80B (Microsoft), $65B (Meta), $75B (Alphabet) and $100B (Amazon). Deals such as Intel's roughly $11B joint venture with Apollo raise capital off balance sheet. Falling model costs (DeepSeek) were flagged as a risk to the economics. CHECK for data center deals:
  - Who is the credit: the hyperscaler parent, or an SPV or JV with a terminable lease?
  - What are the lease term, termination and assignment rights against the debt tenor?
  - What happens to the asset if compute demand per dollar falls?

  [NEW; STRENGTHENS 6.8, 6.9] (Goldman Sachs bankers at Yale, "Who Will Finance the AI Revolution?," Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/who-will-finance-the-ai-revolution)
- **Water for cooling.** Phoenix and Front Range data centers depend on Colorado River-linked water certainty (see 1). Add "water source, priority and cooling design" to the 6.3 data room. [NEW] (Cort, Yale Insights)
- **Public green-bank and subsidy counterparties carry appropriation risk.** In 2018 the Connecticut legislature redirected a substantial part of the Connecticut Green Bank's revenue to the general fund. CHECK: where a program depends on a state green bank, a ratepayer charge or an appropriation, confirm whether the funds can legally be swept, and model their loss. [NEW] (Yale SOM case, "Connecticut Green Bank 2018: Finance and Marketing After the Raid," https://cases.som.yale.edu/connecticut-green-bank-2018)

## 10 Funds and Co-Investments

### 10.1 How it works: the allocator's view

- **Asset allocation drives most return variation, and manager selection drives the rest in illiquid assets.** Swensen cites Ibbotson's finding that asset allocation explains over 90% of the variability of institutional portfolio returns. Ten-year first-to-third-quartile manager spreads to June 2005 (Swensen's figures, from his lecture):

  | Asset class | Spread |
  |---|---|
  | Fixed income | 0.5 pts/yr |
  | Large-cap equity | ~2 |
  | Small-cap | 4.7 |
  | Hedge funds | 7.1 |
  | Real estate | 9.3 |
  | Leveraged buyouts | 13.7 |
  | Venture capital (top vs bottom quartile) | 43.2 |

  Implication: in private markets, choosing a median manager forfeits most of the reason to be in the asset class. [NEW] (Swensen, ECON 252 lecture 9, https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252-08/lecture-9)
- **What Yale looks for.**
  - Managers "maniacally focused on beating the markets," not on gathering fees.
  - Modest fund sizes and willingness to close to new money ("Size is the enemy of performance").
  - Integrity and character weighed alongside numbers.
  - Openness to emerging managers without formal track records. Swensen called the standard pension process, a 50-to-100-page questionnaire plus 5 to 10 years of audited history, "not the richest pond." The Yale Investments Office says many of its longest partnerships "began with talented but unproven managers."

  [NEW] (Swensen, ECON 252 lecture 9; Yale Investments Office, https://investments.yale.edu/partners)

### 10.4 Diligence checklist (additions)

- **Performance persistence is real in PE and stronger in VC, but it decays with fund growth.** GPs whose fund beats the industry tend to beat it in the next fund and the one after (Kaplan-Schoar). Persistence is weaker in buyout because successful buyout GPs scale fund size faster. Endowments have historically outperformed other LP types partly through better fund selection (Lerner et al.). Phalippou argues VC persistence is concentrated among funds backed by less sophisticated LPs. Present both positions. CHECK: compare successor-fund size with predecessor-fund size and team size. [NEW] (Metrick and Yasuda survey, NBER w16652)
- **Track-record data are biased upward. Correct before benchmarking.** Swensen cites:
  - Malkiel's hedge fund estimates: survivorship bias about 4.4% a year and backfill bias about 7.3% a year. About 75% of 1996 funds were gone by 2004.
  - Ibbotson's study of about 3,500 funds: 2.9% and 4.6% a year.
  - A Russell manager universe that fell from 307 to 177 reporters between 1996 and 2005. The year-2000 domestic equity universe return was reported as -3.1% at the time and showed +1.2% in the 2005 database.

  CHECK: benchmark against vintage-matched, survivor-inclusive data. Ask the GP for every fund and vehicle ever managed, including closed and abandoned ones. [NEW; STRENGTHENS 10.5] (Swensen, ECON 252 lecture 9)
- **Dollar-weighted IRR cannot be compounded or compared to time-weighted returns.** Yale reported a 20-year venture capital IRR of 165.9% to June 2018. It explicitly said this should not be compounded because it was dominated by internet-boom distributions; the 20-year time-weighted figure was 24.6%. Yale uses dollar-weighted IRR for illiquid classes because "the managers... determine when to buy and sell." CHECK: never compound an IRR, and never compare an IRR to a TWR index. Use PME or KS-PME for comparison, and show IRR with and without the largest one or two exits. [NEW; STRENGTHENS 10.5] (Yale News, "Investment return of 12.3% brings Yale endowment value to $29.4 billion," Oct 2018, https://news.yale.edu/2018/10/01/investment-return-123-brings-yale-endowment-value-294-billion)
- **Investor dollar-weighted returns can be far below fund returns.** The ten best internet funds of 1997 to 2002 showed +1.5% a year time-weighted, but investors lost $9.9B of $13.7B invested (72%) because the money came in near the top. Morningstar found dollar-weighted below time-weighted returns in all 17 equity categories, with a 13.4-point a year gap for tech funds. For evergreen and semi-liquid funds, request investor-level (dollar-weighted) returns and flow history. [NEW] (Swensen, ECON 252 lecture 9)
- **The private equity premium is cyclical.** Using LP cash flows, the estimated PE premium (alpha over traded factors) was positive in the first half of the sample and turned negative later, with strong cyclicality by fund type. It is consistent with capital-market segmentation (Kaplan-Stromberg). Do not underwrite a constant illiquidity premium. [NEW; abstract-level for the published figures] (Ang, Chen, Goetzmann, Phalippou, "Estimating Private Equity Returns from Limited Partner Cash Flows," JF 2018, https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:73:y:2018:i:4:p:1751-1783)
- **Private fund payoffs are partly spanned by public factors.** Across nearly 5,000 private funds, Goetzmann, Gourier and Phalippou extract latent "private factors," several only weakly correlated with public markets. Benchmark private funds against public factor exposures (size, value, leverage-adjusted equity) before crediting alpha. [NEW; abstract-level, preliminary draft] (Goetzmann, Gourier, Phalippou, "How Alternative Are Private Markets?," NBER w24941, https://www.nber.org/papers/w24941.pdf)
- **Fund terms: full benchmark from Metrick and Yasuda.** See 5 for the revenue split and terms. Add to 10.4:
  - Management fee step-down or basis switch after the investment period. In the sample, 84% of buyout and 43% of VC funds switched to an invested-capital basis.
  - Hurdle and catch-up (6% to 10% band, 8% modal).
  - Carry basis: committed capital (used by 83% of buyout and 92% of VC funds) versus invested capital.
  - Transaction and monitoring fee offset share.

  [STRENGTHENS 10.4, 10.5]

### 10.3 Operational due diligence (additions)

- **ODD is commissioned at the wrong moment, and the market ignores it.** DD reports are typically commissioned after strong performance and inflows. The operational-risk score built from them predicted later fund failure out of sample, yet operational risk did not reduce investor flows. The herd invests anyway. Run ODD to the same standard on a hot manager as on a cold one, and give the ODD team veto power. [NEW] (Trust and Delegation, NBER w15529)
- **Specific ODD fields with base rates** (Trust and Delegation, 444 reports):

  | Field | Base rate |
  |---|---|
  | Wire-signature process disagrees with the administrator | 16% |
  | Pricing process disagrees with the administrator | 3.6% |
  | Major data vendor switched in last 3 years | 11% |
  | DD questions refused | 14% |
  | Assets unverifiable or disagreeing | ~19% |
  | Performance unverifiable or disagreeing | 14% |

  Use these as the comparison set for the ODD scorecard. A typical third-party ODD report then cost about $12,500. [NEW]
- **LPs cannot fire the manager, so the contract is the only governance.** Investors in funds generally cannot remove the management company except for egregious misconduct. LPACs have no day-to-day authority and cannot hire or fire, though they may approve conflicts and sometimes trigger dissolution or removal votes. Limited partners who exercise control risk losing limited liability. Exit rights substitute for control: the weaker the exit (10-year PE lockup), the more the LPA must carry (key-person dissolution, for-cause removal standard, no-fault divorce threshold, LPAC conflict approvals). CHECK: read the removal standard (cause definition, vote threshold, who decides) and the key-person trigger. Treat any fund with neither key-person dissolution nor a workable removal right as having no governance. [NEW; STRENGTHENS 10.4] (John Morley, "The Separation of Funds and Managers: A Theory of Investment Fund Structure and Regulation," Yale Law Journal 123 (2014), https://www.yalelawjournal.org/article/the-separation-of-funds-and-managers-a-theory-of-investment-fund-structure-and-regulation)

### 10.6 Failure modes (new)

- **CASE FILE: a state pension's governance failure (Connecticut, about $40B).** Sonnenfeld and Tian estimate the state would have earned about $5B more over five years, and about $27B more over ten, at the 50-state median return. A plain 60/40 portfolio beat it by more than 1 point a year over the decade. The state cycled through eight CIOs and four interim CIOs. It paid high fees, sometimes to managers where it was the sole or dominant client, and reported fees on total assets (including index funds) rather than actively managed assets. THE CHECK for any allocator or fund-of-funds:
  - CIO tenure.
  - Fees as a share of actively managed assets.
  - Manager-level net performance against a passive alternative.
  - Concentration where the LP is a manager's anchor or only client.

  [NEW] (Jeffrey Sonnenfeld and Steven Tian, "Why Connecticut's Investments Are Underperforming," Yale Insights, May 2023, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/why-connecticuts-investments-are-underperforming)
- **CASE FILE: Reserve Primary Fund.** A "safe" pooled vehicle gated for years. See 0.5. [NEW]

### 10.7 Red flags (add)

- Successor fund much larger than the funds that produced the track record, without a matching increase in senior team. [NEW]
- Track record presented as a compounded IRR, or IRR compared with a time-weighted index. [NEW]
- Sharpe or Sortino offered as proof of skill for a strategy that uses options or leverage. [NEW]
- Manager's account of cash controls differs from the administrator's. [NEW]
- Non-Big 4 or niche auditor combined with internal pricing. In the ODD data, both were significantly associated with misrepresentation. [STRENGTHENS 10.7]

### 11.1 Crypto and digital assets

- **Stablecoins and tokenized cash: a vulnerability framework.** Score each money-like product on five features:
  - Liquidity transformation.
  - Threshold effects (a peg or $1 NAV that invites runs once broken).
  - Use as a money-like asset.
  - Contagion channels.
  - Reactive investors.

  Apply it to any stablecoin, tokenized MMF or "yield-bearing dollar" held as collateral or treasury. [NEW] (JFC, "A Framework for Understanding the Vulnerabilities of New Money-Like Products," JFC 8(3), https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/journal-of-financial-crises/vol8/iss3/1/)
- **Holding the peg under stress is not proof of safety.** Gorton likens stablecoins to free-banking-era private banknotes, which traded at discounts and suffered runs. Tether briefly broke its peg in 2022 and recovered. Reserve reports build reputation but do not remove run risk. CHECK: require reserve composition at the instrument level, its custodian, and redemption terms and timing for non-retail holders. [NEW] (Gary Gorton, "Stablecoins Survived 'Crypto Winter,' But That Doesn't Make Them Safe," Yale Insights, https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/stablecoins-survived-crypto-winter-but-that-doesnt-make-them-safe)
- **CASE FILE: FTX, 2022.** No accounting department, no complete employee list, and customer deposits off the balance sheet. THE CHECK: controls and accounting infrastructure first, then token economics. [NEW] (Antle, Yale Insights)
