# Reviewer checklist (internal): Medical Cannabis Dispensary Acquisition

Not for the sponsor. Pass criteria, verification steps, kill tests and red flags.

> **THE ONE CHECK (Cannabis and Hemp):** Rebuild federal tax at full 280E on every dollar of revenue, then credit relief only for medical revenue backed by a DEA registration under the new expedited pathway, a tax opinion and an audit-proof apportionment; count prior-year refund claims at zero.

> **THE ONE CHECK (Private Equity and M&A):** Recompute the return with exit multiple equal to entry, margins flat and management's forecast haircut about 20%, on EBITDA proven to cash by an independent QoE, and read the R&W policy exclusions against the diligence reports: if the deal no longer clears the hurdle, or known findings are neither priced nor specifically indemnified, it does not pass.

## Kill tests (any one triggered = decline)

- [ ] `U-K01` One person decides on investments, moves cash and reports results, with no independent administrator, custodian or auditor separating those duties. (bible 0.4, 0.17)
- [ ] `U-K02` Sponsor refuses direct, unsolicited confirmation with any bank, administrator, auditor, custodian, prime broker, servicer or processor, or refuses on-site diligence. (bible 0.4, 0.5)
- [ ] `U-K03` Bank, custody or performance statements are available only as sponsor-produced copies and the sponsor will not authorize delivery from the institution. (bible 0.4, 0.5)
- [ ] `U-K04` No accounting function: no in-house CFO or controller, no employee census that reconciles to payroll, or customer assets and matching liabilities not recorded on the balance sheet (the bible calls this an automatic pass). (bible 0.4)
- [ ] `U-K06` Any investor, beneficial owner, borrower or counterparty is on the OFAC SDN or sectoral lists, UN, EU or UK lists, or is owned 50% or more in aggregate by blocked persons; or a crypto address in the chain is sanctioned. (bible 0.3, 0.18)
- [ ] `U-K07` Beneficial owners (every 25% owner and a control person) cannot be identified and verified through our own CDD. (bible 0.3)
- [ ] `U-K08` Source of funds or source of wealth for the sponsor's commitment or any principal cannot be established with third-party documents. (bible 0.3)
- [ ] `U-K09` Distributions or returns to existing investors are funded by new investor money (the definition of a Ponzi), or a fixed or guaranteed above-market return cannot be traced to asset cash flow. (bible 0.4, 0.5)
- [ ] `U-K10` Independent background check finds a material criminal, fraud, securities or regulatory matter that the sponsor or a principal did not disclose in the signed self-disclosure. (bible 0.2)
- [ ] `U-K11` Resume, licence, degree or claimed prior exit of a principal is shown to be fabricated or materially inflated. (bible 0.2)
- [ ] `U-K13` An undisclosed lien, UCC filing, guarantee, factoring or supply-chain-finance facility is found on the issuer, an affiliate or a principal-owned entity and the sponsor cannot explain why it was omitted. (bible 0.4, 0.5)
- [ ] `U-K17` Customer or fund assets are commingled with personal or sponsor accounts, or used for other ventures. (bible 0.4, 0.5)
- [ ] `U-K18` Bank or administrator wire-approval matrix shows a single person can release funds, or contradicts the sponsor's description and the sponsor cannot reconcile it. (bible 0.5)
- [ ] `U-K21` Wire or principal instructions cannot be verified by call-back to an independently obtained number. (bible 0.4)
- [ ] `U-K22` Named validators, partners, customers or co-investors deny the relationship or the diligence the sponsor attributed to them. (bible 0.4)
- [ ] `U-K23` The sponsor refuses to provide 36 months of bank statements for every account, or the bank confirmations show accounts missing from the set provided. (bible 0.5, A.6)
- [ ] `CAN-K01` License cannot be confirmed with the state regulator as real, current, in good standing and transferable (or approvable for the change of control). (bible 8.3, 8.4)
- [ ] `CAN-K02` Projections deduct ordinary operating expenses federally and the operator will not accept a model rebuilt at full 280E. (bible 8.2, 8.4, 8.5)
- [ ] `CAN-K03` Schedule III relief assumed for adult-use revenue, or for medical revenue without a DEA registration, tax opinion and audit-proof apportionment, and value depends on it. (bible 8.3, 8.4, 8.6)
- [ ] `CAN-K04` No seed-to-sale compliance trail. (bible 8.5, 8.3)
- [ ] `CAN-K05` Cash-heavy business that cannot complete proof of cash and segregation-of-duties testing. (bible 8.3)
- [ ] `CAN-K06` License held in a name different from the operating entity with no approved path to align it before closing. (bible 8.5)
- [ ] `PE-K01` Seller refuses a buy-side QoE, proof of cash on every bank account, or customer calls. (bible 5.8, 5.3)
- [ ] `PE-K02` Adjusted EBITDA presented with no bridge from reported net income, or add-backs that cannot be independently validated and the price is not reset to validated EBITDA. (bible 5.3, 5.8)
- [ ] `PE-K03` The return does not clear the hurdle with margins flat, the forecast haircut about 20% and exit multiple equal to entry, and the case depends on multiple expansion or margin expansion with no named cost line. (bible 5.14, 5.8)
- [ ] `PE-K04` Downside case breaches debt covenants or cannot service debt, including all factoring and supply-chain finance added to leverage. (bible 5.4, 5.3)
- [ ] `PE-K05` A known regulatory or data-integrity defect is handled only by the general MAE, with no specific indemnity, closing condition or price adjustment. (bible 5.6, 5.7)
- [ ] `PE-K06` Stock consideration or merger partner not diligenced (no independent QoE on the counterparty, auditor not called), or counterparty's auditor resigned recently without explanation. (bible 5.13, 5.8)
- [ ] `PE-K07` Data-room hints (volume declines, cancellation letters, rebates) remain unasked or unanswered at signing. (bible 5.3, 5.7)

## Requests: pass criteria and independent verification

### Stage 1. Screen: send with your first reply

**`U-STR-001` [GATING] One-page summary stating what the deal is and the ask in one line: amount, instrument, our allocation, use of proceeds, structure and security.**
- Why: A deal that cannot be stated in one line usually hides its structure (0.8 deck order item 1).
- Pass: Amount, instrument, allocation, use of proceeds and security are each stated and tie to the sources-and-uses table to the dollar.
- Fail: Ask, instrument or security missing or inconsistent with the model or term sheet.
- Verify independently: Tie every figure to the sources-and-uses table and the draft term sheet.
- Bible: 0.7 item 2, 0.8, A.1

**`U-COM-001` [CORE] Investor deck in the universal order (ask, problem, asset, sponsor and track record, market and cycle, numbers, structure, returns with sensitivities, risks, terms), with every sponsor claim marked verified (and by whom) or asserted.**
- Why: Claims presented as fact that nobody independently sourced; investment cases resting on pitch delivery rather than evidence (0.12).
- Pass: Every factual claim appears in the claims register with a source document; every 'verified' claim names a third party we can contact.
- Fail: Claims with no source; 'verified' claims with no named verifier; deck cites round price, a marquee co-investor or a national forecast as validation.
- Verify independently: Score the written materials and data room before the live pitch with a fixed rubric and more than one rater; contact each named verifier.
- Bible: 0.8, 0.12, A.1, A.3

**`U-STR-002` [GATING] Sources-and-uses table showing every dollar in and out, including all fees, reimbursements and payments to the sponsor, its affiliates or insiders.**
- Why: Insider steering and fee leakage hidden in use of proceeds (0.4 sales-side tells).
- Pass: Sources equal uses; every payment to the sponsor, an affiliate or an insider is itemized and disclosed.
- Fail: Unitemized 'other' or 'working capital' buckets; undisclosed payments to related parties; repayment of sponsor loans not disclosed.
- Verify independently: Reconcile to the funds-flow memo at closing and to bank statements after funding.
- Bible: 0.4, 0.7 item 2

**`U-VAL-001` [GATING] Live financial model (native Excel, formulas intact) with historicals, a base case and a downside case, and all assumptions on one input tab.**
- Why: Returns that exist only as a PDF output cannot be stress tested (0.6, 0.7 item 5).
- Pass: Model runs, ties to the historical financials, and contains a separately built downside case; no hard-coded outputs.
- Fail: PDF-only model, hard-coded returns, or downside case that is the base case with a small haircut.
- Verify independently: Rebuild the key outputs ourselves from the input tab; reconcile historicals to bank statements in the QoE.
- Bible: 0.6, 0.7 item 5

- Verify independently: Obtain audited statements directly from the auditor with signed authorization; proof of cash in the QoE.
- Bible: 0.2, 0.4, 0.8

- Fail: Expired or inconsistent IDs, refusal to produce ID for a control person or wire releaser, or names that do not match entity filings.

## Hard questions and what a good answer looks like

- `U-HQ01` What are the first three things you look at in a deal like this, and what makes you stop reading?
  - Good answer: A specific, ranked list tied to the deal's failure modes (cash, collateral, sponsor), with a kill test they have actually applied.
- `U-HQ02` What do you ask for that sponsors rarely have ready, and can you give it to us now?
  - Good answer: They produce the A.6 first-request items (36 months of bank statements, bank-confirmed wire matrix, average balances, off-balance-sheet financing list) promptly and completely.
- `U-HQ03` What does a deck in this asset class most often get wrong, and how does yours avoid it?
  - Good answer: Names a concrete error (exit multiple, margin expansion, stale marks) and shows the corresponding sensitivity in the model.
- `U-HQ04` Which numbers did you rebuild yourself and which did you take from management or a seller?
  - Good answer: Lists rebuilt numbers with the source documents, and names the figures taken on trust with the rep that covers each.
- `U-HQ05` Tell us about a deal that looked clean and went bad. What did you miss?
  - Good answer: A specific case, the variable misjudged, and the process change made afterwards.
- `U-HQ06` Which third-party reports have you commissioned, who engaged each provider, and may we speak to them?
  - Good answer: Reports engaged by an independent party, with engagement letters and consent to call the providers.
- `U-HQ07` Which terms are non-negotiable for you, and why?
  - Good answer: Investor-protective terms (information, audit, consent rights) rather than sponsor-protective ones.
- `U-HQ08` Whose diligence are you relying on, and what would make you trust it?
  - Good answer: They rely on no one else's diligence without verifying its scope and hours, and can say what they checked themselves.
- `U-HQ09` How many hours did you spend with management in person, how many sites did you visit, and how many references did you call?
  - Good answer: Specific numbers at or above the floor (about 10 references; meaningful in-person hours), and what was skipped for timing.
- `U-HQ10` Describe a decision process you changed after a loss.
  - Good answer: A concrete post-mortem and the resulting change.
- `U-HQ11` When did you last decline a hot deal or manager on operational grounds alone?
  - Good answer: A dated example with the operational issue that drove the decline.
- `U-HQ12` How do you size the haircut to management's forecast, and how has it compared with outcomes?
  - Good answer: An explicit haircut (around 20% median) calibrated against their own forecast record.
- `U-HQ13` Who set the walk-away price, and is that person on the deal team?
  - Good answer: A walk-away set before bidding by someone separate from the deal champions.
- `U-HQ14` Which of the statements you received were contradicted by third-party records?
  - Good answer: Specific examples and how each was resolved, showing they check against third-party records.
- `U-HQ15` When did you last test a whistleblower hotline, here or at a portfolio company, and what happened?
  - Good answer: They have tested it, it worked, and they can show hotline statistics.
- `U-HQ16` Show us the amendment history of every credit agreement and LPA.
  - Good answer: A complete amendment log with reasons; no serial extensions hiding losses.
- `U-HQ17` How do you dedupe collateral against other lenders' borrowing bases?
  - Good answer: Item-level dedupe (invoice, VIN, serial) across all facilities by an independent collateral agent, plus a UCC sweep across affiliates.
- `U-HQ18` What share of your last ten deals changed CEO, and when did you first know?
  - Good answer: A number near the base rate (69%), with early identification and a successor bench.
- `U-HQ19` Why are you offering this co-investment, and is the main fund taking its full pro rata?
  - Good answer: Main fund takes its full pro rata; syndication is for size or concentration, with a rule-based allocation policy.
- `U-HQ20` Who chose lender counsel on your last three financings, and what did independent counsel change?
  - Good answer: Independent counsel for lenders; specific changes to baskets or EBITDA definitions.
- `U-HQ21` What is your fixed-fee share of revenue in a downside, and do fees keep the lights on without carry?
  - Good answer: A fee base that funds the team in a downside without depending on new fundraising.
- `U-HQ22` Which default definition do you report, and what is your realized loss rate?
  - Good answer: Names the definition (payment, covenant, realized loss) and gives the realized loss with source.
- `U-HQ23` If your bank closed this morning, how many weeks of payroll could you meet?
  - Good answer: A specific number of weeks from cash held in multiple banks, insured sweeps or Treasury MMFs.
- `U-HQ24` Who can release a wire, and will your bank confirm that list to us directly?
  - Good answer: At least two signatories with an independent approver, and immediate consent to bank confirmation.
- `U-HQ25` Who has write access to pricing inputs and valuation models?
  - Good answer: Nobody on the investment team; the administrator or independent valuer controls inputs, with access logs.
- `U-HQ26` What did your QoE not test, and did proof of cash cover every account on the bank confirmations?
  - Good answer: Reads out the scope exclusions and confirms full account coverage.
- `U-HQ27` Who else saw this deal, and did a banker run a process?
  - Good answer: Names the intermediary and other bidders; the claim can be corroborated.
- `U-HQ28` What is your annual deployment target, and what share of the market is that?
  - Good answer: A modest share of the market with a record of declining deals to stay selective.

## Failure cases this packet is built to catch

- **Madoff (exposed 2008)**: About $65B intake, about $18B investor losses; smooth stock-like returns; auditor Friehling and Horowitz was a two-partner firm with no peer review. CHECK: Third-party verification of custody and trades; auditor sized to the mandate.
- **Drive Planning (SEC 2024 and 2025)**: Promised 10% every six months on a 'bridge loan'; principals inflated resumes; COO and a managing partner later charged for calling the return guaranteed and collateral-backed. CHECK: Proof of cash; background check on the whole placement chain; resume verification.
- **Toltec Capital (SEC)**: Principals inflated resumes to lure investors. CHECK: Verify every employer, title, degree and licence independently.
- **Adam brothers (SEC 2024)**: Promised returns up to 13.5% monthly. CHECK: Treat guaranteed or fixed above-market returns as a red flag; proof of cash.
- **Theranos (2003 to 2018)**: Raised close to $1B at a $9B valuation; 2014 revenue projected $100M vs just over $100,000 actual; analyzer ran about a dozen of 200 promised tests; false DoD claim; unauthorized pharma logos; 100-to-1 founder votes and a board without domain expertise. CHECK: Independent technical validation on our own samples; call every named validator and partner; reconcile projections to invoices and cash; map voting control and board expertise.
- **FTX (2022)**: No accounting department, no complete employee list, customer deposits off the balance sheet and routed to Alameda; raised nearly $2B from VCs; FTX raised $400M at $32B months before collapse. CHECK: Controls and accounting infrastructure first; proof of reserves AND liabilities; independent custodian; do not treat last-round valuation as a mark.
- **QuadrigaCX (2019)**: CEO reportedly sole key holder; Ponzi with fictitious balances credited to aliases; at least C$169M client losses. CHECK: Key-ceremony documentation and quorum test.
- **First Brands Group (Chapter 11 Sept. 2025)**: Alleged nonexistent receivables sold to factors, collateral double- and triple-pledged, off-balance-sheet inventory financing through founder-owned entities; leverage about 5x reported vs about 20x actual; $12M cash vs more than $9B liabilities; founder and brother indicted Jan. 2026 (deny). CHECK: UCC search across all affiliates and founder-owned entities; direct verification of a receivables sample with lockbox remittance; reconcile all factoring and SCF to what each lender is told; cash proof at the parent.
- **Tricolor Holdings (2025)**: Alleged double-pledging of auto loans across warehouse lenders and ABS; altered loan descriptions; charged-off loans parked in a fictitious portfolio company; about 40% of roughly 70,000 loans shared VINs or attributes; CFO pleaded guilty; SEC charges Aug. 2026. CHECK: Loan-level (VIN) dedupe across all facilities by an independent collateral agent; custodial possession of titles; static-pool charge-off reconciliation.
- **GPB Capital (convicted 2024)**: About $1.6B from individuals; distributions represented as portfolio cash flow but paid from new capital; founder's sentence commuted Nov. 2025, conviction stands. CHECK: Reconcile every distribution to its source account and to portfolio-company cash flow.
- **Heller / Paramount-Prestige ATMs (SEC 2025)**: Over $770M from about 2,700 investors for fixed monthly 'ATM fee' distributions funded by new money; about $400M losses. CHECK: Confirm operating assets exist and earn the claimed revenue, from the processor, not the sponsor.
- **Archegos (March 2021)**: Concentrated total-return swaps across prime brokers, none seeing the whole book; banks lost more than $10B (sentencing URL WEAK). CHECK: Aggregate exposure across all counterparties.
- **First Liberty Building & Loan (SEC 2025)**: About $140M from about 300 investors at up to 18%; a Ponzi behind a 'few defaults' claim. CHECK: Borrower-level payment history traced to bank deposits and a delinquency roll.
- **LeFever Mattson (SEC 2025)**: At least $46M from about 200 investors; claimed LP interests did not match records. CHECK: Confirm claimed interests against the entity's records and the county recorder.
- **Infinity Q (sentenced April 2023)**: Founder manipulated inputs to 'independent' Bloomberg BVAL models, overvaluing assets by more than $1B and collecting nearly $27M of fees; fabricated valuation-committee minutes. CHECK: Ask who has write access to pricing inputs; administrator price on a Level 3 sample; read minutes for dissent.
- **SQN Capital Management (SEC Oct. 2026)**: Principal personally prepared the only valuations for four funds while claiming an independent expert; allegedly inflated 2019 to 2025. CHECK: Obtain the valuation firm's engagement letter and call the firm.
- **Blue Apron (2017)**: About 70% of acquired customers churned within six months; IPO range cut from $15 to $17 to $10 (WEAK, podcast-sourced). CHECK: Cohort retention curves before the price is set.

- **GE / Alstom (2015 onward)**: $22B impairment. CHECK: Walk-away price; probability-weighted synergies.
- **Kraft Heinz (2015 onward)**: More than $28B of write-downs. CHECK: Walk-away price; brand value tested against velocity, not cost cuts.
- **Microsoft / Nokia (2013 to 2015)**: $7.6B impairment on a $7.2B deal. CHECK: Walk-away price; dis-synergy line.
- **Sprint / Nextel (2005 onward)**: Nearly $30B goodwill impairment. CHECK: Walk-away price; integration and dis-synergy modeling.
- **Quaker / Snapple (1994 to 1997)**: Bought for $1.7B, sold for $300M. CHECK: Walk-away price; distribution and repeat tested before paying for brand.
- **Yahoo / Tumblr (2013 to 2019)**: Bought for $1.1B, sold for about $3M. CHECK: Walk-away price set before bidding.
- **Frank / JPMorgan (2021)**: Strategic acquirer paid $175M for a startup with fabricated users (4.25M claimed vs about 300,000). CHECK: Sample live users in production systems with our own engineer.
- **Riordan, Lewis & Haden / Diabetic Care Rx (settled 2019)**: DOJ named the PE sponsor alongside its portfolio pharmacy under the False Claims Act for financing a kickback scheme; $21.36M settlement. CHECK: Pre-close billing and referral diligence; FMV support for referral-source arrangements.
- **Sun Capital (multiemployer pension) (D. Mass. 2018)**: Sun Capital funds held to be a trade or business under common control with a bankrupt portfolio company, exposing them to withdrawal liability. CHECK: Map multiemployer exposure; structure ownership to avoid 80% common control.
- **Och-Ziff (FCPA) (2016)**: Sponsor-level FCPA enforcement. CHECK: Agent and third-party diligence; disclosure decision before signing.
- **SEC monitoring-fee acceleration actions (2015 to 2016)**: Four affiliated PE advisers fined $12.5M plus $37.5M disgorgement for accelerating monitoring fees on exit without adequate disclosure. CHECK: Read every sponsor fee agreement with the target; flag acceleration on exit.
- **Lehman Repo 105 (2008)**: Up to $50B moved off balance sheet at quarter-ends; the auditor knew. CHECK: Average daily or intra-quarter balances; flag financing that unwinds just after period-end.
- **First Brands (2025)**: Lenders thought leverage was about 5x; counting off-balance-sheet factoring and SCF it was closer to 20x; double-pledged collateral. CHECK: Add every factoring and SCF facility to leverage; UCC sweep; item-level dedupe.
- **Blackstone Argentina investment (pre-2008)**: Dollar debt against a currency peg; lost every dollar. CHECK: Currency-mismatch test.
- **Fortis v. Johnson & Johnson (Auris Health) (Del. Ch. 2024; Del. Supr. Jan. 2026)**: Earnout milestones for a robotic-surgery product; Chancery found breach of efforts obligations and fraudulent inducement of one milestone; Supreme Court partly reversed: the negotiated efforts definition let J&J calibrate effort for the first payment but not the rest. CHECK: The efforts definition is the asset: read it before valuing any earnout receivable.
- **Bank DCF false negative (2011 example)**: A bank's $800M DCF cap lost to an $840M bid that abandonment value would have justified at $849M. CHECK: Value core NPV plus growth option and abandonment value with explicit ranged assumptions, never to rationalize a price.
- **PE-owned nursing homes (study of 1,674 acquisitions)**: Short-term mortality rose about 10% to 11%; nursing-assistant hours fell about 3%; costs shifted to monitoring fees, interest and lease payments. CHECK: Model regulatory and reputational exposure from staffing and quality metrics and fee, lease and interest leakage.
